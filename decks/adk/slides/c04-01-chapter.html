Agentic design patterns

"…finding the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed."

Docs Anthropic, Building effective agents (2024-12-19) Codelab ADK 2 Orchestration: Graph, Collaborative & Dynamic Workflows
This chapter builds four parts of the Acme back office. Acme is the fictional company that runs through the whole course, and every lab is one folder in labs/adk/c04-*, tested on google-adk 2.13.0. Each part is one agentic pattern drawn as a graph: three claim checks fan out and a JoinNode waits for all three; an evaluator-optimizer loop fixes ad copy and stops after three rounds or escalates; an orchestrator starts one auditor per expense line with ctx.run_node; and AgentTool subagents look up part prices. The chip row gives the cost of each shape: a fan-out costs the slowest branch, a loop costs up to two model calls per round, and an orchestrator costs one call per worker. Think of the patterns as plumbing fittings: you pick the fitting that matches the pipe, not the most complex one in the box. A likely question: "Why not start with a multi-agent system?" Anthropic's advice on the slide is the answer: start simple and add a shape only when an eval shows you need it. Next: the map of seven patterns and the ADK feature that builds each one.

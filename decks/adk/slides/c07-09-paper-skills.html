Lost in Implicit Routing: How LLM Agents Fail to Load Relevant Skills at Scale

With 1,000 irrelevant skills, recall falls 73.6% → 27.4%. Zero-load rises 9.3% → 51.6%. Preselection keeps 73.9%.
Above ~50 skills, rank first and pass only the top k.
Paper arXiv 2610.18522
This October paper says a skill library does not scale by adding folders. Click 1: with no distractors the agent loads the needed skill 73.6% of the time; with 1,000 irrelevant skills in the library, recall falls to 27.4%. Click 2: the zero-load rate, where the agent loads nothing although it needed a skill, rises from 9.3% to 51.6%, so about half the tasks run with no skill at all. Click 3: a cheap ranking step in front, preselection, keeps recall at 73.9% with the same 1,000 distractors. The paper's advice is to preselect once you pass about 50 skills, so 1,000 is where it fully collapses, not where the problem starts. A related paper, arXiv 2608.14036, finds that 65.7% of skill wins come from procedural anchoring and that actual-use precision falls from 29.6% to 3.3% as the pool grows from 5 to 100, so write skills as procedures and keep the pool small. Think of a new hire handed 1,000 binders: they read none, so you hand them the three that matter today. A likely question: "Is EAGER discovery enough?" EAGER puts the catalog into the system instruction up front instead of behind list_skills; with a large catalog you still preselect. The build slide after next shows the preselect code.

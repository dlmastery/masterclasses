Step 1: interface drift means the tool schema the model learned is not the schema it gets: renamed fields, new enums, extra tools. Step 2: the paper (arXiv 2610.23342, 16 Oct 2026) wraps each call in a training-free validate, retry and repair layer, SchemaShield-Lite: validate the arguments against the schema, ask the model to retry, repair what is still wrong, then run the tool. On BFCL-200 with Qwen3.5-9B, accuracy rose from 0.580 to 0.805, and distractor tools did most of the damage. The token in the animation is an illustrative argument: "broken" is not in the Literal enum, so validation fails and the repair gives "damaged". Step 3: for Acme, keep the three tools, and put argument checks in before_tool_callback, as refund_cap does. Analogy: a form checker at the counter sends the form back before the clerk files it. Likely question: "Does ADK do this validation for me?" The Literal enum and the mandatory-argument check catch some errors; semantic checks are your callback's job.

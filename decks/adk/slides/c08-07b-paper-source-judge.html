A second paper looked at what goes wrong inside summaries. Press once: a source judge checks each summary entry against the raw event record; "Plan: Family 4-line" is supported by the get_bill output. Press again: "Next: waive roaming fee" has no source in any event, so the judge drops it; in the study, 83 percent of failed runs traced to an action that carried out one such summary entry, and pruning unsupported entries improved cross-run reliability by 2.1 to 8.9 percent at no extra agent tokens. Press again: the judge keeps the forged "always run X" line, because that text really is in a tool output; a source check cannot see who wrote the words. So you need both defences: the judge for invented steps, and escaping or role-typing for forged roles, which the next slide builds. In ADK, write a custom BaseEventsSummarizer, and set event_retention_size high enough that the last raw tool outputs survive. Think of an editor who checks every claim against a source, but cannot tell a forged quote inside a real document. A likely question: "Does the judge cost extra agent tokens?" The paper reports no extra agent tokens; the judge runs at compaction time.

arXiv 2610.15459
When Messages Go Wrong ran over 29,000 executions of multi-agent systems with injected communication faults. Two numbers matter for design reviews. First, 20.7% of failures give no immediate signal, and for partial-content faults, where a message arrives cut short, it is 76.2%: the system keeps going on bad data. Second, the topology decides how much survives: under faults, flat topologies kept 53.0% task success, against 3.3% for sequential and 3.2% for hierarchical ones, when an alternative path kept the evidence. That is the claim-checks fan-out from this chapter: every branch reads the original claim, so one bad message cannot poison the others. The Monday change for Acme: let each fan-out branch read the original input, not a chain of rewrites, and check partial content explicitly. Analogy: three people reading the same letter against a game of telephone. A likely question: "Does this mean chains are always bad?" No, but each link adds a point where a silent fault can enter, so keep chains short and validate between links. The last slides turn the chapter into checks for a design review.

Loop, graph and harness engineering, verifiers, evals and guardrails

Reliability is engineered: bounded loops, owned edges, verified steps and measured runs.

Evals before features. Assume prompt injection works.

loop engineering graph engineering harness engineering evals
This chapter turns a demo agent into a system you can trust. Press four times: each shield is one discipline. The capped loop is loop engineering: every loop gets a stop condition and a budget. The fork with a lock is graph engineering: code owns the edges that must not change. The ring of sensors is harness engineering: every mistake becomes a new check around the agent. The test tube is evals: you measure the whole run before and after each change. The two lines under the title are the short form of the 2026 vendor guidance: write evals before features, and assume a prompt injection will succeed. Analogy: a bridge engineer does not hope the bridge holds; she adds load limits, joints, sensors and inspections. Likely student question: "Is this only for large teams?" No. A two-person team needs a budget cap, one verifier and a small eval set more than anyone, because nobody watches the agent at night.

Most public MCP servers have no OAuth, and many vanish in days

arXiv 2608.00150 2026-07-31

Exposed by Design: A Dynamic Security Assessment of Internet-Facing MCP Servers at Scale

A live audit of 414 internet-facing MCP servers. 91.8% lack OAuth. 68 vulnerabilities, including SSRF to cloud metadata.
Connect only to remote servers with OAuth. Keep metadata hosts blocked in production.
Paper arXiv 2608.00150
Three papers from the last three months change how the Acme desk picks and runs MCP servers; this is the first. Step 1: the authors found 414 MCP servers on the public internet and tested them live, not from their code. Step 2: 91.8% of them have no OAuth, so anyone who finds the URL can call their tools. Step 3: they found 68 vulnerabilities, including SSRF to cloud metadata, the same class of attack that the OpenAPI guard blocks, and 41.6% of the servers were gone within three days, so a public server is also unstable. Step 4: what to change on Monday: connect only to remote servers that use OAuth, pin and review what you connect to, and keep metadata hosts blocked in production. Think of a public MCP server like a stranger's USB stick: useful, maybe, but you check it before you plug it in. A likely question: "Does ADK support OAuth for MCP?" Yes, McpToolset takes auth_scheme and auth_credential, and ADK asks for the credential with adk_request_credential before it lists tools.

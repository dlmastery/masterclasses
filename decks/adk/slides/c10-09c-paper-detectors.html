Injection detectors ranked on BIPIA do not transfer to AgentDojo

arXiv 2610.034482026-10-02

Passing the Test You Trained On: Re-evaluating Prompt-Injection Detectors for LLM Agents

The best BIPIA detector catches 2% of AgentDojo injections at 1% FPR. 72% on AgentDojo became 15% on tau-bench. FPR does transfer.
Replay your own tool outputs through Model Armor. Measure FPR first.
The third paper, from October, explains why the decision belongs in code and the classifier is only the second layer. Press once: four injection detectors, ranked by their score on the public BIPIA benchmark; detector A is the leader. Press again: re-rank them by what they catch on AgentDojo at a 1% false-positive rate, and the table flips: the BIPIA leader catches 2% of AgentDojo injections, and the best AgentDojo detector, at 72%, falls to 15% on tau-bench. The one number that does transfer between benchmarks is the false-positive rate. The bars for B and C are schematic; A and D are the paper's numbers. Press again: so measure on your own data: replay real Acme tool outputs through Model Armor and measure its false-positive rate before you turn on blocking. Analogy: a student who memorised last year's exam does not pass this year's. Likely question: "So is Model Armor useless?" No: it is a useful second layer on prompts, replies and tool outputs, but its catch rate on your traffic is unknown until you measure it, while the origin check from two slides ago holds by construction.

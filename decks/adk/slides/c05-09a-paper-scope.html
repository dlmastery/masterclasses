Two papers from September and October say the same thing: a yes from a human is only as safe as the scope of that yes. The grid is the 2610.22987 result, not an Acme measurement: one tile is one percent of that paper's capability tests for coding agents, and the approval card on the right is our Acme example. First click: residual authority replay reuses an earlier approval for a new action and raises attack success by up to 35.1 percentage points on AgentDojo, a prompt-injection benchmark. So scope each approval to one interrupt_id and never write "approved" into user: state; a second order, A-2041, gets a new interrupt and a new pause. Second click: in From Approval to Abuse, planted repo context got coding agents to request broad allow-rules in 58% of instances; those rules covered 56.9% of capability tests, and once a rule was approved, 47 of 58 runs reached silent malicious completion. Analogy: a signed cheque names the payee and the amount; a blank signed cheque is a standing rule. A likely question: "Does the ADK gate already scope approvals?" Yes, if you keep the lab's id per pass and never copy the yes into state.

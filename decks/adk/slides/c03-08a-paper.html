An October paper on the cheapest part of a support graph to optimise: the routing decision. Jev-LiteMAS measured three multi-agent frameworks. Step 1: routing and review take 31.7 to 43.5 percent of all generative-LLM tokens, so the router is often a large part of the bill. Step 2: replace the generative router with a lightweight decision model, which returns a calibrated probability instead of text, and the total generative tokens fall to 45 to 87 percent of the baseline, a 12.7 to 54.6 percent cut, with accuracy within 1.34 points. The paper adds a condition: "Grounding routing in substantive expert outputs prevents up to 18.0 points in accuracy loss", so the decision must read the real expert outputs. Step 3: for Lab 3 the same idea needs no new model: keep classify on a flash model with a Literal schema, and let the route_ticket function decide. Analogy: a triage desk with a checklist is cheaper than a doctor who writes an essay for every patient. A likely question: "Do I need the Jev model in ADK?" No; the labs ClassifierNode exists in 2.13, but a Literal classifier plus a function gives the same separation. Next: the second paper warns where this can backfire.

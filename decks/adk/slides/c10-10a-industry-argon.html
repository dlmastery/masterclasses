Post Optimizing a 200,000-line Python codebase overnight
The best engineering case for ADK comes from the ADK team itself: they let Gemini 4 Argon work on the ADK Python codebase for 11 hours, with a goal prompt that asked for an unmerged pull request with before and after numbers, guided by a SKILL.md index in .agents/skills. Press once: it built a baseline of 4 workloads with 30 runs each and proposed 6 changes across 32 files. Press again: the maintainers shipped 2 and rejected 4; one rejected change passed all 16,892 open-source tests but broke 25 internal Google test targets, and a 17% cache gain held only on a synthetic benchmark. Press again: the two shipped changes, a 21% faster cold import and about 40% less session preparation, made a 158-turn run drop from 65.6 to 56.1 seconds; the post adds an ops tip that fits our canary: warm up each new instance before it takes live traffic. Analogy: a junior engineer who works all night is useful, but a senior still reviews the pull request. Likely question: "Would our tests have caught the rejected change?" Only if they cover what the internal targets covered; that is why a human review and a canary stay in the loop.

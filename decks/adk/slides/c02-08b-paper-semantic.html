def check_claim(claim: dict) -> None:
    big_loss = claim.get("estimated_loss_usd", 0) > 10_000
    hurt = claim.get("injuries_reported", False)
    if claim["severity"] == "high" and not (hurt or big_loss):
        raise ValueError("severity=high needs injury or > $10k loss")
# in main(), right after get_session:
check_claim(claim)

Paper slide, September 2026. Click 1: constrained decoding forces the model to emit tokens that fit the schema, and schema validity in small models went from 78.6-92.9% to 100%. The bar shows the band and the jump. Click 2: but the meaning can still be wrong, and the paper finds that this semantic gap depends on model scale. Our example: a valid "high" with no injury and a $6,400 loss passes the schema and is still wrong. Click 3: what to change on Monday: check_claim runs in your code right after get_session and raises ValueError when severity is high without an injury or a loss over $10k; it is in claim_intake.py in the lab folder. Analogy: a form with a box for each answer still lets you write the wrong answer in the right box. A likely question: "Does output_schema not validate this already?" It validates types and enum values, not business rules, so the business rule lives in your code.

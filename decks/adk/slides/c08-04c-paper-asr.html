Misheard numbers cost voice agents 22-26 points of tool accuracy

arXiv 2610.17619 2026-10-13

When Does an ASR Error Matter? Task-Role Diagnostics for Tool-Using Voice Agents

ASR edits to numbers and negations cost 21.9-25.7 pp of tool-call accuracy. Control edits cost 1.4-2.9 pp.
Re-confirm account numbers and amounts in the tool.
arXiv 2610.17619 · ASR task-role diagnostics
This paper tested cascaded voice agents, where speech recognition turns audio into text before the agent picks a tool. Press once: in the Acme call, the risky words are the account number and the amount, not the small talk. Press again: when the authors changed numbers and negations in the transcript, tool-call accuracy fell by 21.9 to 25.7 percentage points; control edits of other words cost only 1.4 to 2.9 points. Press again: the action is to make the tool re-confirm account numbers and amounts, for example by reading the last four digits back, before it changes anything. Analogy: a waiter who mishears "no nuts" makes a worse mistake than one who mishears "please". A second paper, Heartbeat Thinking (arXiv 2610.26233), cut final-response latency by 36.4 percent by starting read-only tool calls while the caller still speaks. A likely question: "Does a native-audio model avoid ASR?" It removes the separate ASR step, but the model still has to hear the digits correctly, so keep the check in the tool.

Why compile: context rot

Chroma tested 18 LLMs. Accuracy drops as the input grows, even on simple tasks. n tokens give n² attention pairs (Anthropic).

Poisoning

A hallucination enters the context and gets re-cited.

Distraction

Long history outweighs what the model learned in training.

Confusion

Irrelevant material, such as too many tools, steers the output.

Clash

Parts of the context contradict each other.

Google ADK: "the Session is your ground truth; the working context is merely a computed projection."

Start with the why: Chroma tested 18 models and every one got worse as the input grew, and Breunig's four failure modes name how it goes wrong. Then press through the compiler. Sources on the left are durable storage; the processors are ordered passes; the card on the right is the only thing the model sees in this one call, and the gauge shows the tokens falling at each pass. Step 1 is templating (Select), step 2 is include_contents='none' (Isolate), step 3 is the compaction summary (Compress), step 4 freezes the stable prefix for the cache, and step 5 writes the result back through output_key (Write). Analogy: a compiler does not ship your whole repository to the CPU; it emits one small binary per target. Notes on templating: {key?} is the optional form, and since ADK 2.10 ${var} is no longer filled from state. Likely interview question: "Name the four failure modes and one ADK fix for each": poisoning gets node isolation, distraction gets compaction, confusion gets fewer tools or skills, and clash gets one source of truth in state.

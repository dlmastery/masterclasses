In production Uber

Uber writes AI spend as six terms and pulls levers on two

Tokens per request: less reasoning, earlier compaction.

Price per token: cheaper subagents and longer cache hits.

Budgets nudge people; they do not block the work.

Post Running a Software Factory Efficiently at Uber Scale
Uber writes its AI bill as six terms multiplied together, and every cost lever attacks one term. Users, sessions, turns and requests grew on purpose, so they are greyed out. Press once: two levers cut tokens per request: reasoning effort defaults to Medium, and auto-compaction runs at 400K tokens even on 1M-context models. Press again: two levers cut the price per token: subagents default to a cheaper model, which Uber calls the most impactful lever, and the interactive cache TTL went from 5 minutes to 1 hour, because idle gaps kept invalidating the cache, exactly the TTL build-up from this chapter. Press again: budgets without hard caps run across every term: a live cost counter, Slack nudges at 50, 80 and 100 percent, and a dashboard that flags 16 anti-patterns. Think of a phone bill: you cannot stop people calling, but you can change the tariff and the length of each call. A likely question: "Why no hard caps?" Hard caps block real work, so Uber makes the cost visible instead.

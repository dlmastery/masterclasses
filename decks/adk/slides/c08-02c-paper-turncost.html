TurnCost: turn count, not observation size, drives agent cost

arXiv 2610.15093 2026-10-10

Turns, Not Observations: Cache-Aware Loop Engineering for Coding Agents

98.6% of input tokens came from the prefix cache. Turn count explains 84% of cost variance; observation size explains 4%.
Keep the prefix stable. Cut the number of turns.
arXiv 2610.15093 · TurnCost
TurnCost studied the cost of coding-agent loops, and it explains why the cache lab matters. Press once: 98.6 percent of all input tokens were served from the prefix cache, so a stable prefix is not a small optimisation, it is most of the bill. Press again: when the authors explained cost variance, the number of turns explained 84 percent and the size of tool observations only 4 percent; every turn re-sends the whole window, so each extra turn costs a full prefix read. Press again: the action for Monday is to keep the static prefix byte-stable and to cut turns, for example by letting one tool call do the work of three. Analogy: a taxi meter that charges per stop matters more than the length of each stop. A likely question: "Is this only for coding agents?" The study is on coding agents, but the mechanism, a long cached prefix re-sent every turn, is the same in the Acme support agent.

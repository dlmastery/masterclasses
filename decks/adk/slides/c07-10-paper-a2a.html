Support triage delegates refunds to an agent that another team owns, so its reply crosses a trust boundary. Click 1: the peer sends text as data, but triage's LLM reads it as an instruction, and A2ABreak shows the A2A spec itself has 11 protocol-level holes, so authenticate every hop. Click 2: a study of 2,500 runs found that no agent checks a peer's facts unless you make it, so triage gets its own read-only order lookup, for example refund_core.lookup_order as a tool, and A-200 is electronics at day 45, which contradicts the claim. Click 3: the CaMeL paper shows the core fix: a separate instruction lane and data lane. In ADK terms, state does not cross RemoteA2aAgent, so the peer cannot write into our state; instead triage parses the reply text on its own side, with a local agent or callback that has an output_schema, into state["refund_verdict"], and code, the RefundGuardPlugin from step 6, applies the $500 cap. Click 4: attack success falls from 12.9% with no defence to 0.2% with per-agent CaMeL and 0.0% with the two channels. Think of a contractor's invoice: you pay against your own purchase order, not against a note written on the invoice. A likely question: "Is 0.2% not good enough?" It is close, but it gives no guarantee across the hierarchy.

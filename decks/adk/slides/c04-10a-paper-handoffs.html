arXiv 2608.16055
Governance at the Boundary puts a number on the cost of decomposing an agent. The authors ran 626 KYC and AML episodes in their Fiducia benchmark. With a 32B open model, a policy fact such as a PEP match, a politically exposed person, never reached the component that had to act on it in 0% of single-loop runs, 56% of pipeline runs and 85% of orchestrator-plus-subagent runs; gpt-4.1-mini lost only 3 to 6%. The animation is a relay race: every handoff is a chance to drop the baton, and a single runner cannot drop it. The lanes are schematic; the numbers on the right are the paper's. The change for Acme on Monday: pass policy facts as typed state, with output_schema and output_key, for example a risk_flags list, and end the graph with a deterministic check node that reads that state. With small models, keep compliance-critical paths in one agent. A likely question: "So should I never use an orchestrator?" Use one when the eval says it pays, but measure fact loss at each boundary.

Build the Acme support desk as a graph: a typed chain, a router, and the same router in YAML.

"The graph model is worth the learning curve as soon as you have more than two branching paths."

Docs Google Cloud, I/O '26 news for agent developers (2026-05-19) Acme support desk tested on google-adk 2.13.0
This chapter builds the Acme support desk as three graphs; press three times to preview them. Step 1 is the refund chain: parse the request, check the 30-day policy, write the reply, write an audit line, always in that order. Step 2 is the triage router: the model labels the ticket from a closed set, and a plain function picks the route, so a P1 outage always pages on-call. Step 3 is the same router as YAML that a PM or a lawyer can review. After the lab: porting a 1.x SequentialAgent, two gotchas (a silent route failure and two loud graph errors), one paper on what routing costs, and a rules slide. Think of the graph as the rail track and the model as the driver: the driver sets the speed, but the track decides where the train can go. A likely question: "When do I need a graph instead of one agent with tools?" Google's I/O '26 advice is the quote on the slide: as soon as you have more than two branching paths.

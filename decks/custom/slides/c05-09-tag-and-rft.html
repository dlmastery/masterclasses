def tag_reward(r_primary, aux, lambdas):     # r_primary in {0,1}

arXiv 2610.21404 Multi-Reward Hacking (TAG) arXiv 2610.25812 Is it RL or Data?
Two October papers change how we write the claims reward. The first, Multi-Reward Hacking, shows that a plain sum like correctness plus 0.1 times brevity gives the wrong-but-short cell a positive reward, and inside a GRPO group that small 0.1 still beats 0, so the policy drifts toward short wrong payouts. TAG multiplies the auxiliary credit by correctness, so a wrong payout earns exactly zero; on Qwen3-4B with a brevity reward this gave pass@4 0.581 versus 0.220 and 44% shorter answers. The second paper keeps prompts, verifier and token cap fixed and finds GRPO beats tuned rejection-sampling fine-tuning by up to 10.9 points, while pass@64 barely moves: RL sharpens what the base model can already do. Analogy: GRPO is a coach who makes your best shot reliable, not one who teaches a new shot. Likely question: "Should I skip RFT entirely?" If you have a verifier, run GRPO first; if you must use RFT, iterate it and resample from the current policy each round.

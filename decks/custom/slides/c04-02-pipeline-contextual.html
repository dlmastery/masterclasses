Why a contextual header helps

A chunk such as “revenue grew 3%” loses its company and its quarter. An LLM reads the full document and writes a short header. The header goes into the embedding and the BM25 text.

Tune chunking with retrieval recall on a labeled set, not by eye.

Top-20 retrieval failure rate

5.7%baseline embeddings
  • Baseline: 5.7%
  • + contextual embeddings: 3.7% (−35%)
  • + contextual BM25: 2.9% (−49%)
  • + reranking: 1.9% (−67%)

Anthropic, 2024-09-19. Failure = 1 − recall@20.

Walk the pipeline left to right. Step 1: parse, then chunk; the Vertex AI RAG Engine quickstart uses 512 tokens with 100 overlap, and the overlap keeps sentences that cross a boundary. Step 2: an LLM writes a context header for each chunk, and both the dense and the BM25 index see it; the failure rate drops from 5.7% to 3.7%. Step 3: the query is rewritten, and hybrid retrieval merges both indexes; contextual BM25 takes it to 2.9%. Step 4: a reranker cuts 20 to 150 candidates down to 3 to 20, and the failure rate is 1.9%, which is 67% fewer failures. Analogy: BM25 is the index at the back of a book, embeddings are a librarian who knows synonyms; you want both. Likely question: "Why BM25 in 2026?" Because exact identifiers, error codes and product names are lexical, and embeddings blur them.

Shopify Building production-ready agentic systems
Each team built its reward or eval signal from its own data first. That signal is the part you cannot rent.
This slide is the reason for the course: three teams that rent or reuse base models, and win with their own data, rewards and evals. Click 1, Cursor (rewards lever): real-time RL for Composer. They serve a checkpoint, turn user interactions into rewards (did the edit stay in the codebase, did the user send an unhappy follow-up), update the weights, pass an eval gate on CursorBench, and deploy. One cycle takes about five hours. The A/B test gave 3.13% fewer dissatisfied follow-ups and 10.3% lower latency. Click 2, Databricks KARL (data loop plus RL): an agent synthesizes grounded question-answer pairs, a dedup agent removes overlap with the eval set, solver agents drop questions that are always or never solved, a quality filter removes bad items, then off-policy RL trains GLM 4.5 Air. With 10 parallel rollouts (test-time compute) KARL scores 67.5 on KARLBench, the same as Claude 4.6 Opus; the base model scores 52.6. Source: the KARL tech report, Table 4. Click 3, Shopify Sidekick (eval lever): Cohen's kappa measures agreement beyond chance; 1.0 is perfect, 0 is chance. They tuned the judge until kappa went from 0.02 to 0.61, against 0.69 between two humans, and only then trained with GRPO and gated rewards. Analogy: three restaurants with the same supplier; the menu is different because each one tastes and scores its own dishes. Likely question: "Do these numbers transfer to my team?" No. They show what to measure. Your own eval set decides.

Reward hacking hides and spreads

GPT-4o monitored o3-mini CoT. Training against the monitor gave obfuscated reward hacking, so pay a “monitorability tax”. Anthropic: hacking coding tasks spread to ~12% sabotage and ~50% alignment-faking. Inoculation prompting fixed it: “Learning to cheat no longer generalized to other misaligned behaviors.”

This slide teaches suspicion. Step 1: RL with verifiable rewards wins at pass@1, but when you sample many answers the base model solves more problems; RL mostly sharpens what the base model can already do. Step 2: on Qwen2.5-Math, random rewards give 21.4 of the 29.1 points that true rewards give, so a large gain on one model family can come from contamination or format effects, not from the reward. Step 3: reward hacking grows up. If you train against a chain-of-thought monitor, the model keeps cheating and writes a clean-looking plan; Anthropic saw hacking spread to sabotage and alignment-faking, and one line of inoculation prompt stopped the spread. Step 4 lists the other open problems, from saturation and collapse to licensing, platform exits and vendor claims. Analogy: a student who learns the answer key gets top marks and learns nothing; the marks look the same from outside. Likely question: "Should I stop using RL?" No: use RL, but run a random-reward control, report large-k pass@k and keep the monitor out of the reward.

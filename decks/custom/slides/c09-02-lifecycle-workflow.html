One workflow runs design, generate, select, curate, label, denoise and the quality card for every release

1 Design

contract lint

Write the taxonomy, slices and contract first: ODCS v3.1.0 with datacontract-cli, or Pandera 0.34.1 in Python.

2 Generate

provenance flag per row

Mix logs with synthetic rows: distilabel Magpie, personas, teacher distillation (TRL DistillationTrainer).

3 Select

removal rate logged

Score with a FineWeb-Edu-style classifier. Target with DSIR or LESS: the LESS 5% often beats the full set.

4 Curate

cross-split overlap = 0

MinHash + semantic dedup, then decontaminate. datatrove 0.10.1; NeMo Curator 1.4.0 GPU fuzzy dedup: 10.7 h → 0.65 h.

5 Label

α ≥ 0.8

Experts accept or fix LLM suggestions in Label Studio 1.23.2. Measure agreement before you scale.

6 Denoise

audit rate on card

Find label issues with cleanlab 2.9.0. Run verifiers and unit tests on every synthetic row.

7 Train + eval

no slice drops beyond CI

TRL v1.16.0, Unsloth or Vertex tuning. Inspect AI 0.3.283, lm-eval 0.4.13, DeepEval 4.3.1, promptfoo, Vertex client.evals.

8 Card + monitor

card ships with release

Data Card + Croissant-RAI + eval card. Evidently 0.7.23 tests drift on descriptors.

A drift alert goes back to design: update the taxonomy, publish contract v2, add the new slice.
Tools stop. Argilla: maintenance only. distilabel: community-maintained. Snorkel: no release since 2024, 3 CVEs. HELM: maintenance mode since 2026-06-01. WhyLabs hosted: shut down. torchtune: wound down in 2025. Pin versions, keep open formats (ODCS, Croissant, JSONL) and keep recipes portable.

Walk round the wheel one pair of stages per step. Each stage makes an artifact, uses a named tool and ends at a gate that CI can check: the contract lints, every row has a provenance flag, every filter logs its removal rate, no item appears in two splits, agreement reaches alpha 0.8, and no slice drops beyond its confidence interval. Training and evaluation sit in the hub, because every stage feeds them. Step 5 closes the loop: a drift alert from monitoring goes back to design, and the next release starts with a new taxonomy leaf and contract v2. Step 6 is the honest part: many tools in this space stop. Argilla and HELM are in maintenance mode, Snorkel has open CVEs, and WhyLabs closed its hosted product. Analogy: a building keeps its plans in a standard format, so a new builder can continue the work. Likely question: "Which gate do teams skip most often?" The cross-split overlap check, and it is the one that inflates the eval most.

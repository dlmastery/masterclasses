Choose the lever

Facts → context. Behaviour → weights. Eval decides.

This chapter makes one decision before anyone rents a GPU: which lever fixes which failure. Press through the diagram. The Acme refunds assistant gives a wrong answer, and the first step is not a fix: it is an eval of 20 or more real cases, with each failure tagged. A missing fact, such as "14-day window" when the policy says 30 days, is a knowledge failure, so it goes to context and RAG (retrieval-augmented generation). A missing refund_amount field is a behaviour failure, so it goes to SFT (supervised fine-tuning on example answers) or RL (reinforcement learning against a reward score). After each fix you re-run the same eval. Analogy: a new support agent who does not know the refund policy needs the handbook; one who knows the policy but writes messy replies needs coaching. Likely student question: "Can one fine-tune fix both?" It can try, but slide 6 shows that SFT on new facts raises hallucination, so facts stay in retrieval.

# claims_data.py - procedural insurance-claim payout problems with exact gold answers
import random
from datasets import Dataset
# raw string: "\b" is a backspace otherwise
SYSTEM = r"You are a claims adjuster. Think briefly, then give the payout as \boxed{number}."

def make_claim(rng):
    damage = rng.randrange(500, 20000, 50)
    deductible = rng.choice([250, 500, 1000])
    share = rng.choice([70, 80, 90, 100])            # % insurer pays after the deductible
    limit = rng.choice([5000, 10000, 15000])
    payout = min(max(damage - deductible, 0) * share / 100, limit)
    q = (f"Damage ${damage}. Deductible ${deductible}. Insurer pays {share}% after the "
         f"deductible, capped at ${limit}. What is the payout in dollars?")
    return {"prompt": [{"role": "system", "content": SYSTEM}, {"role": "user", "content": q}],
            "solution": f"{payout:g}"}               # accuracy_reward reads `solution`

def claims_dataset(n, seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    return Dataset.from_list([make_claim(rng) for _ in range(n)])

r"..." keeps \boxed intact. Without r, \b is a backspace (gotcha after Lab 4).
Three valves in a fixed order. The formula is the gold, so the reward has no label noise.

Lab 4 starts with data, and for RL the best data is a generator whose answers are exact. make_claim draws a damage amount, a deductible, a coinsurance share and a policy limit, and the payout is one line of arithmetic, so the gold solution has no label noise and the reward cannot be wrong. Watch the pipe: the damage bar shrinks by the deductible, scales by the share, and the 15400 claim hits the 5000 cap, which is exactly the step a weak model forgets. Analogy: it is a maths teacher who writes the exam with a calculator beside her, so every answer key is right. Likely question: "Why the raw string on SYSTEM?" Without the r prefix, the \b in \boxed is a backspace character, so the model is told to answer in a garbled format that accuracy_reward cannot read; the research verifier found this bug. Train on seed 7 and hold out seed 99, and keep a mix of easy and hard claims so groups do not all score the same.

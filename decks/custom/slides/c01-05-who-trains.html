Five production teams, one shape. Inside your harness a router sends each request either to a swappable frontier model or to a specialist that the team trained for one workflow. The specialists are peers of the frontier model, not copies trained from it: Chroma started from gpt-oss-20b, Ramp from a 35B model, Intercom from an undisclosed open-weights base. Cursor runs a five-hour RL loop on live traffic; Vercel trained a fixer that writes 8,130 characters per second against 559 for gemini-2.5-flash; Chroma and Ramp trained retrieval and search subagents. Intercom is the large case: hundreds of billions of parameters, still for one workflow, and its number is vendor-reported. Vercel is from June 2025; the other four are from 2026. Think of a hospital: the general physician stays, and you add a specialist clinic for the case you see every day. A likely question: "what is the router in my ADK app?" It is plain code or a small classifier that picks the model per request. Another: "why not fine-tune the frontier model itself?" Speed, cost and portability: the base keeps improving, while your specialist, data and evals stay yours. For Acme, the specialist is the refunds assistant, trained only for the format failure.

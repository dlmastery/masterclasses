Measure coverage against a taxonomy, and turn every gap into a named slice

Taxonomy = domain × task × skill × difficulty × language × format × risk category. Tag every row with its leaf ID.

Design the tree first, then generate per leaf

GLAN (Microsoft, 2024): the only input is a taxonomy, from fields to disciplines, subjects, syllabus, key concepts and instructions. To add a skill, insert a node. #InsTag (2023): 6.6K open-set tags. TagLM, tuned on 6K tag-selected samples, beats models tuned on far larger SFT sets on MT-Bench.

Beyond counts: embedding coverage (the share of production clusters with training mass), nearest-neighbour distance from production queries to find holes, and instruction diversity (InsTag tags, distinct n-grams, self-BLEU, semantic dedup ratio).

slices/shipping_customs.yaml (illustrative)

slice: shipping_customs
predicate: "domain == 'shipping' and task == 'customs'"
min_eval_n: 200
metric: [task_success, groundedness]
gate: "task_success >= overall - 0.03"
owner: support-ml@company
Find the slices you did not declare. Slice Finder (ICDE 2019) searches for large, interpretable failing slices with statistical tests. Domino (ICLR 2022) clusters cross-modal embeddings: it finds 36% of 1,235 planted slice settings (+12 pp) and names 35% exactly. Then cluster failing production traffic and promote recurring clusters to declared slices.

Coverage is a question about a taxonomy, not about a row count. Step 1: the tree for a support bot, three domains and twelve task leaves; GLAN shows that a taxonomy alone can drive generation, and InsTag shows that tag diversity predicts SFT value better than size. Step 2: each leaf becomes a row of a heatmap with train, dev and test columns; the colour is the example count. Step 3: a cell under k = 30 is a hole. Here 9 of 12 leaves are covered, the normalized entropy of the train mix is 0.83, and exp(H) says the data behaves like about 8 equal leaves, not 12. Step 4: a hole is not a to-do note; it becomes a named slice with a predicate, a minimum eval n of 200, a metric, a gate and an owner. Step 5: declared slices are not enough, so use discovery; Domino finds only 36 percent of planted slices, which tells you discovery is a help, not a guarantee. Analogy: the taxonomy is the map, the heatmap shows where you have roads, and a slice is a named road with a speed limit and an inspector. Likely question: "How big should k be?" Size it from the confidence interval you need on the eval split; 30 to 100 per leaf is a common start, and 200 for a slice gate.

Three recent papers say the same thing about preference tuning: the numbers in the DPO log are not the quality of the replies. ECHO shows the mechanism on the left: chosen and rejected hidden states drift together by a shared offset, the relative loss does not see it because it cancels in the difference, and the unembedding turns that offset into one token whose probability keeps growing. Ranking accuracy goes up while external reward models like the samples less, so read generations at every eval; note that the strongest collapse is under their per-token ranking loss, and textbook DPO kept the offset weak at their budgets. The hyperparameter paper says the best LR moves with model size and does not transfer across datasets, so run short LR by beta sweeps on your own downstream eval; the heatmap is schematic, only the fact that the best cell moves comes from the paper. Analogy: a student who learns to pick the better of two answers on the test but starts to repeat one word in every essay. Likely question: "Is my Lab 5 run at risk?" Read ten Acme replies at each eval and watch the distinct-token share; if it falls while margins grow, stop.

  1. Normalize the text. Shingle it into word 5-grams.
  2. MinHash the shingle set into a signature (128 permutations). The chance that two signatures match at one position equals their Jaccard similarity s.
  3. Split the signature into b bands of r rows. Two documents are candidates if any band matches:
    P = 1 − (1 − sr)b · threshold ≈ (1/b)1/r
  4. Verify the Jaccard of each candidate pair. Find the connected components. Keep one document per cluster.

from datasketch import MinHash, MinHashLSH
def mh(text, num_perm=128, n=5):
    toks = text.lower().split()
    m = MinHash(num_perm=num_perm)
    for sh in {" ".join(toks[i:i + n])
               for i in range(max(1, len(toks) - n + 1))}:
        m.update(sh.encode("utf8"))
    return m

Is the benchmark in the training data?

  • Measure per benchmark (Sainz et al., EMNLP 2023 Findings). Contamination can lead to “incorrect scientific conclusions”.
  • GSM1k: a fresh clone of GSM8k with matched difficulty. Accuracy drops by up to 8% for some families. The chance to generate GSM8k items correlates with the gap (r² = 0.36).
  • Min-K% Prob: the mean log-prob of the k% least likely tokens. It needs no reference model; +7.4% on WikiMIA.
  • LiveCodeBench tests each model on problems released after its cutoff. BIG-bench embeds a canary GUID so corpus builders can filter it.

Checklist for SFT, RL and RAG projects

  • Decontaminate SFT and RL sets against every eval set: n-gram + MinHash + embeddings.
  • Keep a private, time-stamped holdout. Report the training cutoff with each score.
  • Check that RL reward problems do not overlap benchmark problems.
  • Write the method, thresholds and number removed in the quality card.

Duplicates are the cheapest leakage to find and the most common. Lee et al. found one 61-word sentence more than 60,000 times in C4, and over 4% of standard validation sets overlap their training data. Step 1 builds a MinHash signature: each cell matches between two documents with a probability equal to their Jaccard similarity. Step 2 is the banding trick: with 4 bands of 4 rows, the S-curve switches near 0.71. Step 3 morphs to the FineWeb setting, 14 bands of 8 rows, and the threshold moves to about 0.72. Steps 4 and 5 walk the datasketch code; tell students to index the test set and query the training set. Step 6 moves to LLM contamination: GSM1k showed drops of up to 8% for some model families, and LiveCodeBench avoids the problem by dating every problem. Analogy: banding is a set of locks on one door; more rows per band make each lock stricter, more bands give more chances to open. Likely question: "Why not compare all pairs exactly?" Answer: n squared pairs is impossible at web scale; LSH turns it into bucket lookups.

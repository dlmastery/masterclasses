Where Groups Split: A Probe Prior for Nonzero Within-Group Variance in RL Post-Training

Zero-variance groups waste rollouts. A short probe run predicts which prompts stay informative better than the base pass rate.
Sample the base model 8x per prompt; drop 0/8 and 8/8 prompts
Windows: math-verify uses multiprocessing timeouts there, a "huge performance penalty". Run RL on Linux, WSL2 or Colab.
These are the four numbers to watch in the GRPO log of the Acme claims run; the key names are the real ones from TRL 1.16.0, and the values are illustrative. Step 1: rewards/accuracy_reward/mean must rise, here from 0.31 to 0.66; if it stays flat, the policy is not learning. Step 2: frac_reward_zero_std is the share of groups where all 8 answers got the same score, so their advantage is zero and they give no gradient; if it climbs toward 1.0, the prompts became too easy or stayed too hard, and you re-mix the difficulty in the generator. Step 3: completions/clipped_ratio counts answers that hit the 256-token cap; if it rises, raise the cap or make the length penalty stronger. Step 4: entropy should fall slowly; a fast drop means the policy collapses onto one answer pattern. Analogy: frac_reward_zero_std is a class where every student got the same mark, so the teacher learns nothing about who needs help. Likely question: how do I keep that fraction low before training? Sample the base model 8 times per prompt and drop prompts at 0/8 and 8/8; the October paper "Where Groups Split" shows a short probe run predicts informative prompts even better than the base pass rate.

Two production teams do the same thing before any RL step: they measure how often the base model already solves each task and throw away the tasks it always or never solves. Databricks KARL synthesizes grounded question-answer pairs with a vector-search agent, decontaminates them, lets several solver agents try each one, drops the all-solved and none-solved questions, and only then runs OAPL off-policy RL; KARLBench rose from 52.6 to 58.9, and with 10 parallel traces it matches Claude 4.6 Opus at about 33% lower cost. Microsoft ThinkingBox uses a Jeffreys posterior and keeps tasks between 0.08 and 0.90; 50 steps on 187 tasks lifted pass@1 from 51.70% to 60.78% and cut the cost per dependable task from $24.36 to $8.00, but pass@20 fell slightly. Analogy: a gym coach skips the weights you cannot lift and the ones you lift without effort, because neither makes you stronger. Likely question: "Why drop the never-solved tasks?" In a GRPO group they give all-zero rewards, so zero advantage and no gradient, and many of them are broken tasks, which KARL sends to a quality filter.

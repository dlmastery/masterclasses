The dataset is the spec: design it before you generate it

The model learns what the data covers. Gaps in coverage become gaps in capability, and missing provenance becomes legal risk.

Data Cards Croissant 1.1 ODCS v3.1.0 Pandera 0.34.1 Great Expectations 1.24.1 Evidently 0.7.23

In this chapter: document the data for people, machines and CI. Make the contract executable. Measure coverage against a taxonomy and name the gaps as slices. Then compare live data with a reference to find drift.

This chapter opens the data half of the course. The strip shows the full lifecycle that the next chapters follow: design, generate, select, curate, label, denoise, and the quality card at the end. We stay in the first cell, design, because every later step inherits its mistakes. Analogy: the dataset is the building plan; generation and labeling are the construction crew, and a crew cannot fix a plan that has no room for the kitchen. Step 1 shows the current tools with their October 2026 versions. Step 2 is the plan for the chapter: documentation, contracts, coverage and slices, then drift. A likely student question: "Why design first when synthetic data is cheap?" Because cheap generation fills the leaves you asked for and nothing else; the taxonomy decides what gets generated.

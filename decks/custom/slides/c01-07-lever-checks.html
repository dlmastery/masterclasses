These eight checks run before anyone books a GPU, in the same order as the chapter. Eval: start with 20 to 50 eval cases from real Acme refund failures, and tag each failure as knowledge or behaviour. Context: the missing policy facts go to context or RAG, as Databricks Genie Code did to gain 4.5 points at half the cost. RL gating: AWS says RFT requires that the model can produce at least one correct solution among several attempts; that is pass@k greater than zero, where pass@k means at least one of k sampled answers is correct. AWS also suggests a small validation run of 100 to 200 examples before a full RFT job. A base-model screen (arXiv 2610.10478) predicts which base will rank best on SWE-bench after RL, so you choose the base first. On-policy distillation means the student samples and the teacher grades every token; Thinking Machines reached 74.4% on AIME'24 with 1,800 GPU-hours, against 67.6% and 17,920 GPU-hours for RL. The don'ts: do not SFT new facts unless you curate them, do not start RFT when reward is already above 95% because Bedrock says it might be unnecessary, and do not lock your data to one vendor. Think of it as a doctor's triage: you do not operate before you read the chart. A likely question: "how do I check pass@k in practice?" The next slide runs that probe on the Acme base model.

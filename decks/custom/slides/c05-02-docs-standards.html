Document a dataset three ways: a card for people, Croissant for machines, a contract for CI

1 · Card: for people

  • Datasheets for Datasets (2018; CACM 2021): seven question groups, from motivation and composition to collection, labeling, uses, distribution and maintenance.
  • Data Cards (Google, FAccT 2022): 20+ deployed cards. Playbook: Ask, Inspect, Answer, Audit; 15 themes.
  • Hugging Face card: README.md + YAML (license, task_categories, tags). New in 2026: rl-environment.

2 · Croissant JSON-LD: for machines

  • JSON-LD on schema.org: metadata, resources, RecordSet / Field, semantics.
  • 1.1: machine-actionable provenance and usage policies. -RAI: labeling, safety, fairness.
  • Exported by Hugging Face, Kaggle, OpenML.

3 · Contract: for CI

Schema, semantics, SLAs and a change policy as code. CI runs it on every batch and stops the build when it fails. Pandera, Great Expectations, Soda, dbt, ODCS (next slide).

One dataset needs three documents, because it has three kinds of readers. Step 1: the card is for people. Datasheets for Datasets borrowed the idea from electronics, where "every component, no matter how simple or complex, is accompanied with a datasheet". Google's Data Cards and the Hugging Face README card are the formats in use today, and in 2026 the Hub even tags RL environments as datasets. Step 2: Croissant is the same facts as JSON-LD, so tools can load, search and audit a dataset; version 1.1 adds machine-actionable provenance and usage policies. Step 3: the contract is the part that CI runs, which is the next slide. Step 4 is the history in one line. Step 5 is the reason: the Data Provenance Initiative audited more than 1,800 text datasets and found license omission above 70 percent, and EU AI Act Article 10 asks for representative, documented data. Do not quote the Article 10 application dates without a check on EUR-Lex; they moved. Analogy: the card is the user manual, Croissant is the barcode, the contract is the factory inspection. Likely question: "Which one do I write first?" The contract, because you can generate most of the card and the Croissant metadata from it.

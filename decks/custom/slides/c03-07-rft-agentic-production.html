Start with the cleanest production case: Cursor Tab ships a checkpoint, collects accept and reject signals from over 400M requests a day, and updates the policy in about 1.5 to 2 hours. The result is fewer suggestions with a higher accept rate, because the policy targets an accept rate, not a raw count. Then move to agentic RL: one rollout is a whole trajectory of model turns, tool calls and observations, and the reward comes at the end. Mask the tool-output tokens from the loss, because the model did not write them. Rollout lengths have a long tail, so frameworks run rollouts asynchronously and feed a buffer. Analogy: a restaurant kitchen does not stop all cooks while one slow dish finishes. Likely question: "Why mask observation tokens?" Answer: they come from the environment, so their gradient would teach the model to predict tool outputs instead of choosing actions. Treat every managed RFT number here as vendor-reported.

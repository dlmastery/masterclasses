Chapter 8 is Lab 7 on the Acme refund-fraud model, and it answers four questions before the model holds a single refund. First, did October traffic drift away from September after the holiday promo, and which drift statistic should we believe? Second, does our validation leak customers or the future: the same model and data give ROC-AUC 0.886 with shuffled KFold and only 0.602 with GroupKFold. Third, are the scores calibrated, and fourth, which hold threshold minimizes dollars when a manual review costs $15, a paid fraud costs $120, and only 2% of production refunds are fraud. Analogy: a scale that reads the wrong weight, a ruler measured on the training set, and a price tag that nobody computed. Likely question: "Why not just pick 0.5?" Because 0.5 is a probability, not a business decision; step 5 shows the tuned threshold saves 31% and the eval-set threshold costs 66% more in production.

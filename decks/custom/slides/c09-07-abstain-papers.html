This IT-ticket paper from 22 October runs the same protocol as our Lab 8 router: costs 0, 1 and 5, select the threshold on a calibration split, freeze it, and evaluate once on test. Steps 1 to 3 animate its headline: always-route sends 15.3% of tickets to the wrong queue, and a frozen 0.60 threshold on TF-IDF plus logistic regression cuts cost from 0.763 to 0.472 per ticket and wrong routes to 3.5%; the 40 tokens are a schematic, the numbers are the paper's. Step 4 is the warning: GPT-4o-mini few-shot gives one confidence value for every ticket, so correct and wrong tickets cannot be separated and correctness AUROC is 0.500; isotonic recalibration lowers ECE but does not fix that ranking. Steps 5 and 6: monitoring-by-betting watches unstructured text continuously with controlled false alarms, and augmentation on noisy labels amplifies the noise, so run cleanlab first. Analogy: a thermometer stuck at one reading cannot tell a cold from a fever. Likely question: what if my LLM's AUROC is 0.5? Then no threshold helps; use a trained classifier's probabilities, or get a usable confidence signal first. Next: how Wayfair, Amazon and Mastercard apply the same route-or-defer idea to labelling.

The Lab 10 card's judge scored κ 0.58 and failed the 0.60 gate. Audit it on a random sample.

Two production audits show that a judge is an instrument you must calibrate, not an oracle. MagicSchool AI runs 21 judge evaluators on a K-12 product where real failures are rare, so at 0.6% precision analysts saw about 167 flags for every real failure; three levers fixed it: a panel of repeated runs that flags only when every run fails, softer rubrics that require unambiguous and pervasive problems with simpler checklists, and a must-fail canary set that gates every rubric edit, which cut confirmed false flags by 99% and still caught 100% of egregious failures. The text-to-SQL team found its gpt-4o-mini judge had kappa 0.04 on the enriched set; prompt fixes reached only 0.37, a self-hosted Qwen3.6-27B reached 0.72 at about 1/300 of the cost of Claude Opus 4.7, and three strong judges with unanimity routing reached 0.79. Analogy: a smoke alarm that rings at every piece of toast gets unplugged, so tune it but keep a test candle that must always set it off. A likely question is why not add the old judge to the ensemble: the paper found that pairing a weak judge with a strong one lowered agreement.

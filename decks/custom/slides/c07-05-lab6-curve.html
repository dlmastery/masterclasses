This is the payoff of Lab 6: the script prints PR-AUC on a fixed held-out test set after each round of 100 adjuster labels, averaged over 5 seeds. Step 1 draws both curves from that exact table; both start at 0.546 because they share the 100-claim seed set. Step 2: take random's best score, 0.622 at 900 labels, and slide left; the margin curve is already at 0.620 with 300 labels, so the adjusters need about a third of the time, 3 days instead of 9 at 100 claims a day, on this synthetic task. Analogy: a tutor who only asks about the questions you are unsure of learns your weak spots three times faster than one who asks at random. The gotcha: when labels are very noisy, the least-sure claims are often the mislabeled ones, so keep the random 20 in every batch, which also gives you an honest prevalence estimate. Likely question: does the 3x hold on real claims? Not by default; rerun this comparison on your own pool, and for LLM data read Hidden Cartographer, which budgets annotation and training separately.

Labels are measurements: check their agreement, their noise and their splits

Test sets contain errors, annotators disagree, and leaked splits inflate scores. Measure each one before you trust a number.

2,916 label errors in the ImageNet validation set (Northcutt et al. 2021). Confident learning flagged them. Humans confirmed about half of the candidates.

This chapter treats labels as measurements. A measurement has error, and we must measure the error before we trust the result. Step 1 flips three tiles: in a real benchmark, ImageNet validation, about 6% of the labels are wrong, 2,916 images. One of the flipped tiles is the real confident-learning example: missile images that carry the parent label "projectile". Step 2 shows the tools of this chapter: agreement statistics, LLM annotators, cleanlab, active learning with BADGE, group-aware splits and MinHash deduplication, and fresh test sets such as GSM1k. Analogy: a thermometer that reads 2 degrees high still gives a number, but every decision you make with it is wrong. Likely question: "Six percent is small, why care?" Answer: on corrected labels, model rankings can flip, so a small error rate can change which model you ship.

Put each criterion on the scale. Freshness, corpus size and citations with per-user ACLs all push toward RAG: the facts live outside the weights, so you update an index, not a model. Output format and latency push toward fine-tuning: a small tuned model with short prompts is faster and cheaper, and OpenAI's Icelandic example shows that RAG can even hurt a behavior task (87 down to 83 BLEU). Step 6 balances the beam: when you need both, combine them, as Balaguer et al. did (+6 then +5 points), or train the generator RAFT-style with distractor documents (arXiv 2403.10131). Also remember the cost view: fine-tuning adds training, hosting and a deprecation risk when the base model goes away. Analogy: RAG is a reference library, fine-tuning is a training course; a good expert uses both. Likely question: "Can fine-tuning teach new facts?" It can, slowly, and Gekhman et al. show that hallucination rises when it does — keep facts in retrieval.

from datasets import concatenate_datasets, load_dataset
from peft import LoraConfig
from trl import RewardConfig, RewardTrainer
acme = ...  # omitted: Lab 5 pairs x 50, QA scores 1-9, "margin" = QA score gap

uf = uf.map(lambda x: {"margin": float(x["score_chosen"] - x["score_rejected"])})
uf = uf.select_columns(["chosen", "rejected", "margin"])

split = train.train_test_split(test_size=0.05, seed=0)

                    model_init_kwargs={"dtype": "bfloat16"},
                    max_length=1024,                    # longer pairs are dropped

                    eval_strategy="steps", eval_steps=100)
trainer = RewardTrainer(model="Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct", args=args,
    train_dataset=split["train"], eval_dataset=split["test"],
    peft_config=LoraConfig(r=16, lora_alpha=32, target_modules="all-linear",
                           task_type="SEQ_CLS", modules_to_save=["score"]))
print(len(trainer.train_dataset))                   # did max_length drop rows?
trainer.train(); print(trainer.evaluate())
merged = trainer.model.merge_and_unload()           # GRPO loads RMs by path
merged.save_pretrained("support-rm-merged")
trainer.processing_class.save_pretrained("support-rm-merged")

Docs TRL Reward Trainer
Lab 5 aligned the assistant, but DPO gives no score for a new reply. This build adds a learned reward for cases where no rule can check the answer, such as tone: the Acme QA team scores the Lab 5 replies from 1 to 9, and the score gap becomes the margin. Step 1 shows two silent mistakes: a classifier with two labels raises a ValueError, and LoRA without modules_to_save leaves the new score head random, so the reward is noise. Step 3: the margin column makes strongly preferred pairs separate by more, and center_rewards_coefficient keeps the mean reward near zero, so reward weights in GRPO keep their meaning. Step 4: merge the adapter, save a plain checkpoint, and pass its path to GRPO with beta 0.02 and a cheap verifiable guard; takes_action is a deliberately simple keyword check, so keep its weight low. Think of the reward model as a new judge who must learn the score sheet before the contest starts. A likely question: which reward model should I pick? The 22 October paper says do not trust static accuracy; run a short RL job with each candidate and compare the policies.

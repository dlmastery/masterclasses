GRPO scores answers against their group, so cheap partial credit wins

GRPO samples 8 completions per prompt. Advantage = (r − group mean) / group std, scale_rewards="group".

A 0.5 that costs zero tokens beats a 0.0 in its group. Gate partial credit on validity.

gate: not REQUIRED <= obj.keys() → 0.0

All 8 equal: advantage 0, no gradient. TRL logs it as frac_reward_zero_std.

GRPO has no value model: it samples 8 completions for one refund prompt, scores them, and each completion's advantage is its reward minus the group mean, divided by the group standard deviation; that is scale_rewards="group", the TRL 1.16 default. Step through it: two correct answers get big green arrows, the zeros get small red ones, and the hack in card 3, which scored 0.5 under our first draft without any amount or reason, sits above the mean of 0.31, so GRPO pushes the policy toward it. With the schema gate it scores 0.0 and its arrow turns red. In the last step every rollout scores 0.0, the standard deviation is 0, so all advantages are 0 and that prompt gives no gradient at all; TRL counts it in frac_reward_zero_std. Analogy: a teacher who grades on a curve rewards whatever beats the class average, even a blank page with the right heading. Likely question: "Then is partial credit bad?" No, graded credit gives variance when nobody is fully right, but it must be gated on a valid answer, as arXiv 2610.21404 shows for multi-reward GRPO. Next, Lab 3 writes this gated reward in code.

WildTrace: Evaluating Training Data Detection in Realistic Large-Scale LLM Post-Training

Across SFT, DPO and RL stages, the best detection method averages AUC 0.529. That is close to random (0.5).
Record source, license and hash at ingest. You cannot infer provenance later.
arXiv:2610.28707 20 Oct 2026 · verifier-confirmed

SimHack: Predicting the onset of detectable reward hacking before RL training

Predicts the hacking-onset step to 11.3% median relative error. Its curricula train 96.6% longer without hacking. But training-time interventions "incentivize models to obfuscate". RH-Detect (2610.10947): best detector AUROC 0.962 pooled, 10.7-15.9 pp lower on multi-turn tool use.
Log cheap hack detectors from step 0, e.g. code/hardcoded_return_rate.
arXiv:2609.34198 28 Sep 2026 · + TestJack 2610.10619

Frozen Judges, Moving Agents: Version-Dependent LLM-Judge Error

Judge error changes with the agent version. Reusing the old calibration raises comparison error from 3.8 to 19.5 pts. TestJack: ~34.4% of "correct" coding trials violate requirements (50.6% → 33.2%).
Re-calibrate the judge for every new model version. Audit a random sample of passes.

Portable data, unit-tested rewards and a gated quality card are the parts you own.

This slide lists three problems that the field has not solved in October 2026, and the habit that protects you from each one. Step 1: WildTrace shows that after SFT, DPO and RL you cannot tell from the model which data it saw, the best detector averages AUC 0.529, so provenance must be written down when the data comes in. Step 2: SimHack can predict when reward hacking starts to within 11.3% median error and find curricula that delay it by 96.6%, but pushing against hacking during training can teach the model to hide it, and RH-Detect detectors lose 10.7 to 15.9 points on multi-turn tool use, so log cheap detectors from step 0. Steps 3 and 4: a judge calibrated on agent v1 makes 19.5 points of comparison error on v2 instead of 3.8, and TestJack finds that about a third of "passing" coding trials break the requirements, so re-calibrate per version and read a random sample of passes. Analogy: a speed camera calibrated for cars gives wrong readings for motorbikes, you re-calibrate when the vehicle changes. Likely question: "Which of these can my team fix today?" None of the research problems, but all three habits are scripts you already wrote in this deck: the data manifest, the reward unit tests and the quality-card gate.

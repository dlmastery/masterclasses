Steps 1 to 4: four hand-written completions go through both rewards; the good answer gets 0.2 and 1.0, the wrong amount gets 0.2 and 0.5, and the hack and the broken answer get 0.0 and 0.0, which is the exact output on the CPU. Step 5 is why this test exists: our first draft had no REQUIRED gate, and the hack that only says approve and then lists every decision earned 0.5, and inside a GRPO group a free 0.5 beats a 0.0, so the policy would learn it. Step 6: test TRL's own built-ins the same way; think_format_reward wants the completion to start with a think tag, and the soft overlong penalty is a linear ramp from 384 to 512 tokens. Analogy: a known hack in the test suite is like a fire drill, you prove the alarm rings before the real fire. Likely question: "How many hack cases are enough?"

Lesson for the Acme review queue

  • Route by predicted value: direct, reasoning or human.
  • Keep a label only when two cheap judges agree.
  • Send the rest to people.
Three industry papers from August and September show the same pattern for labelling: do not send every item to the most expensive rater. Wayfair's Differential Reasoning Router predicts, per item, whether the direct model, the reasoning model or neither will be right; it calls the reasoning model only when it adds value and sends likely double failures to humans, which saves more than 60% of reasoning tokens. Amazon's QUORUM routes between human and LLM annotators from difficulty features and reports up to 34.4% better quality at 8.8% lower cost. Step 2 adds Mastercard's gate: two local LLM judges must agree, with separate thresholds for positives and negatives, which gave 96.88% purity at 81.7% coverage, while extra reasoning effort added latency and no F0.5 gain. Analogy: a clinic sends routine cases to a nurse, hard cases to a specialist, and asks two nurses to agree before they skip the doctor. Likely question: which signal should the Acme router use? Use cheap, observable difficulty features and model agreement, not the LLM's own stated confidence, which the previous slide showed can be flat. For Lab 8 this means: send the 200-ticket cleanlab queue first to two cheap judges, and give only their disagreements to the team lead.

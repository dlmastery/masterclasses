Interview: AI eval design

Model B beats model A by 1.1 points on 500 items. Do you ship?
Not yet. Compute a paired bootstrap CI of the per-item delta. Unpaired at n = 500, you can detect only about 7 points. Check every slice for regressions. If items come in groups, cluster by conversation or passage.
How do you trust an LLM judge?
Experts label a calibration split. Measure TPR, TNR and κ of the judge against them. Mitigate position, length and self-preference bias. Freeze and version the judge prompt. Correct the pass rate with Rogan-Gladen.
Pick the threshold for a PII filter where a miss costs 20× a false alarm.
Calibrate first. Then t* = 1/(1 + 20) ≈ 0.048. Compute the expected cost at the production prevalence, not at the enriched eval prevalence. Check the threshold per slice.
What goes on an eval quality card?
Set version and owner. Provenance mix. n, slices and the min n per slice. Decontamination method. Label agreement and noise audit. Judge model, prompt version, TPR / TNR. Headline score with CI, worst slice, calibration (ECE), operating point, known gaps.

Ask each question and let the room answer before you show the model answer. Q1 tests statistical hygiene: a 1.1 point gap at 500 items is inside the noise until a paired test says otherwise, and a slice can still regress. Q2 tests whether the candidate treats the judge as an instrument that needs calibration, not as an oracle. Q3 tests decision thinking: calibration first, then a cost-based threshold at the real prevalence. Q4 tests reporting discipline: no number without n, CI, slice and version. The numbers at the bottom are the ones to remember. Likely follow-up: "How many items do you need to detect a 2-point delta?" Do a power analysis: the detectable effect is about 2.8 times the standard error of the delta, so solve for n, and use pairing to cut it.

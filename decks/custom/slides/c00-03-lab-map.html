Analogy: think of the course as a consulting engagement. Acme has four client teams and ten tickets; each lab closes one ticket for one team. Click 0: the rule for the course order. Chapter 01 has no lab, because it decides which lever to use (weights, context or data, from the hero slide). So Lab N runs in Chapter N + 1: Lab 4 is in Chapter 05. Click 1, Labs 1–2, SFT: the refunds assistant learns answer format from 2,992 real refund chats (the Bitext customer-support dataset, REFUND rows); the claims extractor turns claim text into JSON with 4-bit QLoRA on Qwen3-4B. A T4 runs both if you set fp16 (a T4 has no bf16). Click 2, Labs 3–5, rewards and preferences: Lab 3 writes reward functions and unit-tests them on a CPU; its last two steps send the same reward to the OpenAI grader API and to Vertex AI, so they need keys. Lab 4 trains GRPO on claim payouts with an exact-answer reward. Lab 5 trains DPO on chosen versus rejected support replies. Click 3, Labs 6–8, data quality on a CPU: Lab 6 writes a schema contract for claims intake; experts can label only 100 claims a day, so the model picks which rows go to them. Lab 7 checks drift and leakage, then sets the fraud alert threshold from costs: a missed fraud costs $120, a wrongly held refund costs $15. Lab 8 finds drift and wrong labels in ticket triage and sends unsure tickets to a human. Click 4, Labs 9–10, synthetic data and eval: Lab 9 generates HR policy test questions, then dedups and verifies them and checks the LLM judge. Lab 10 builds the release quality card: pass rate per slice with confidence intervals, used as a CI gate. Likely question: "Must I do the labs in order?" No. Each lab has its own data; the order only follows the course.

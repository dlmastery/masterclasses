Start with the Acme failure: the refunds assistant gives wrong policy numbers, and the reflex is to fine-tune them in. Read the left side as the method of the August paper. One SFT target answer holds 10 claims. A probe asks the base model about each claim 8 times; a claim is kept only if at least 5 of the 8 probes recall it, and the target is rewritten without the dropped claims. The first paper shows that SFT targets with facts the base does not know drive hallucination, and Recall Rewrite reduced factual hallucination on the WildHalu and Biography tests. The dots and the bar are an illustration of the method, not the paper's data. Think of SFT as teaching a style of answering, not as filling a library: the library is retrieval. The honest counterpoint is the July paper: if you must bake documents into a LoRA adapter, curation into short canonical answers moved closed-book accuracy from 57.7% to 85.7%. A third paper, SFT-as-Context (arXiv 2610.11132), is about keeping general skills after SFT; we cover it in the SFT chapter on forgetting. A likely question is "how much does hallucination drop?": the paper reports the reduction per benchmark; read the tables before you quote a single number.

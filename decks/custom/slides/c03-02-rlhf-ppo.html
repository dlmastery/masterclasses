“outputs from the 1.3B parameter InstructGPT model are preferred to outputs from the 175B GPT-3”

  1. Fine-tune the base model on human demonstrations (SFT).
  2. Train a reward model on human rankings. Loss: −log σ(r(x,yw) − r(x,yl)).
  3. Run PPO against the reward model. A per-token KL penalty keeps the policy near the SFT model. PPO-ptx mixes in pretraining gradients to reduce the alignment tax.

Walk the three InstructGPT steps one by one. Step 1 gives a starting policy, step 2 turns human rankings into a scalar score with the Bradley-Terry loss, step 3 optimizes that score with PPO while a KL penalty holds the policy near the SFT model. Then show the cost: four models in memory, and about 80% of the time spent on generation, which is why every modern framework uses vLLM for rollouts. Analogy: the reward model is an exam marker trained on a few hundred marked scripts; a student who practises only against that marker learns its blind spots. Likely question: "How do you know when to stop?" Answer: track a held-out gold signal (human or stronger judge) and stop near its peak, not when the proxy reward stops rising.

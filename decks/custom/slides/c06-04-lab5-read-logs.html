This is the last step of Lab 5: run the script and read the DPO log in order, then read the replies. Step 1: the loss starts at ln 2, 0.69, because the policy and the reference are the same model at step 0; if it stays at exactly 0.6931 the adapter changed nothing, which is the LoRA coverage gotcha from chapter 3. Step 2: the margin of 1.14 is made of a small fall of the chosen reward, minus 0.21, and a large fall of the rejected reward, minus 1.35; the TRL docs say DPO works mostly by suppressing the dispreferred reply, so a slowly falling logps/chosen is normal, and a fast fall means you raise the sft weight or beta. Step 3: accuracies near 1.0 within 50 steps on three seed pairs repeated 100 times is memorization, not alignment, so check the eval split. Step 4: the ECHO paper from 21 October 2026 shows that ranking metrics can improve while one token takes over decoding, so read ten samples at every eval. Analogy: the margin is a scoreboard; you still have to watch the game. Likely question: why does the chosen log-prob go down at all? Because the loss only sees the difference, and pushing the rejected reply down is the cheaper way to grow it; the SFT term on the chosen reply limits this. Hand-off: DPO gives Acme an aligned assistant but no score for a new reply; the next slide builds that score as a reward model.

Model-based filters throw away about 90% of the web and still win

DCLM: one fastText classifier beat every other filter

DCLM-Pool is about 240T tokens. The positives are OpenHermes 2.5 and top r/ExplainLikeImFive posts. The filter keeps the top 10%.

Core score: fastText 30.2 · perplexity 29.0 · AskLLM 28.6 · RefinedWeb 27.5

DCLM-7B gets 64% MMLU after 2.6T tokens. Llama 3 8B gets 66% with 6.6x more compute. “model-based filtering is key”.

Distill an LLM judge into a tiny classifier

A large LLM labels about 0.5M documents on a rubric. A small classifier learns the labels. Then the classifier runs over trillions of tokens at a low cost.

Nemotron-CC: filter less, rephrase more

6.3T tokens from a classifier ensemble and synthetic rephrasing. Its 8B model gets +5 MMLU over Llama 3.1 8B. The authors say that a 90% cut hurts long training horizons.

fineweb_edu_score.py · from the model card

from transformers import AutoTokenizer
from transformers import AutoModelForSequenceClassification
tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(
    "HuggingFaceTB/fineweb-edu-classifier")
model = AutoModelForSequenceClassification.from_pretrained(
    "HuggingFaceTB/fineweb-edu-classifier")
text = "This is a test sentence."
inputs = tokenizer(text, return_tensors="pt",
                   padding="longest", truncation=True)
outputs = model(**inputs)
logits = outputs.logits.squeeze(-1).float().detach().numpy()
score = logits.item()
result = {"text": text, "score": score,
          "int_score": int(round(max(0, min(score, 5))))}
keep = result["int_score"] >= 3   # the card advice

Step 1: two funnels. DCLM keeps 3.8T of about 240T tokens, and FineWeb-Edu keeps 1.3T of 15T. Step 2 is the recipe that every lab now copies: Llama-3-70B-Instruct labels 460k pages for educational value, an embedding model with a linear head learns those labels with F1 82% at a threshold of 3, and 6,000 H100 hours score all 15T tokens. Step 3 is the reason: FineWeb-Edu reaches 33.6% MMLU with 38B tokens, where Matrix needs about 300B. Step 4 is the real classifier code from the model card; note the clamp of the score to 0 to 5 and the round. Step 5 is the counter-intuitive part: global dedup left about 4T tokens and scored worse than per-snapshot dedup with 20T, so more cleaning is not always better. Analogy: a sieve that is too fine also removes the gold dust. Likely question: "Why a linear head and not the 70B model?" Cost: the 70B model labels 460k pages once; the tiny head then scores trillions of tokens.

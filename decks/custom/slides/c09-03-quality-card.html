The quality card is the last stage of the lifecycle and the first input of the next release. The card fills one group of fields per step. Step 1: where the items come from, how many there are, and how they were decontaminated. Step 2: who labeled them, how much the labelers agree, and how well the judge matches the experts. Step 3: the headline with a bootstrap interval, and the worst slice, which is often the real story. Step 4: calibration and the operating point, which turns a score into a decision. Step 5 adds the training-data fields that FineWeb and Llama 3 report: removal rate per filter, classifier F1, verifier pass rates and known biases. The left column shows where the idea comes from, from Datasheets in 2018 to OlmoTrace, which links outputs to training documents live. Analogy: a nutrition label lists each ingredient and its amount; a single "healthy" sticker tells you nothing. Likely question: "Who writes the card?" CI writes most of it from the contract, the profiles and the eval run; the owner writes intended use and known gaps.

Q1 rewards the candidate who names the reasons for a workflow: the path is data-determined, the refund cannot be undone, and it needs an audit trail; the mini-graph has exactly one LLM node, classify, and code everywhere else. The "more than two branching paths" line is a heuristic from Google Cloud's I/O '26 post, not a rule. Q2 is the number to know: 0.95 to the power 20 is about 36%; the red mark shows why real systems do worse: measured per-step reliability is below 95%, so in 2609.01660 every model is near zero by 16 dependent steps, where the 0.95 curve still shows 44%. Q3: tau2-bench is a customer-service agent benchmark, and a "cell" is one model in one domain; a centralized multi-agent system lost in 12 of 13 cells, and the bar chart shows why: policy facts are dropped at each hand-off, 0% in a single loop, 56% in a pipeline, 85% with an orchestrator and subagents. Analogy: every hand-off between agents is a game of telephone, and the policy is the message that gets lost. Likely student question: "Is 95% per step realistic?" It is generous for a free LLM step, which is why you add cheap verifiers after the risky ones.

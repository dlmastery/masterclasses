Customizing models: the questions interviewers ask in 2026

39 questions: 13 Q&A slides, 5 whiteboards, 5 debug drills. Talk only; the labs are in the main Customizing deck.

Five chapters act on one system.

context-only: prompt + retrieval + memory, no weight change. Also more accurate: 76.6% vs 72.1%.

LoRA r=16: rank-16 adapters. q,v: attention query and value layers. The 4.4× ratio transfers.

gated: extra rewards count only if the answer is correct. pass@4: solved in ≥ 1 of 4 tries.

GroupKFold: each customer in one fold. AUC: ranking quality, 0.5 = chance.

pinned judge: fixed model version, temperature 0. Close calls flip ~40%.

The loop: a failed eval sends you back to 01, often via 04.

This deck is a drill, not a lecture: 29 slides, of which 13 are Q&A slides with 3 questions each, plus 5 whiteboards, 5 debug drills and the chapter openers. For each question, read it aloud, wait ten seconds, let the room answer, then reveal the model answer. A good answer works like a lab report: it names the mechanism, gives one number and says how you would check it. At the start only "Your system" shows. Clicks one to five each add one chapter, its verb and its number. 01, "the lever", is the choice between prompt, retrieval or context, fine-tuning and RL: Databricks Genie Code cost $0.55 per task against $1.09 for the best general coding agent tested, at 76.6% versus 72.1% accuracy, and Databricks credits workspace context (semantic search, persistent memory) with the same tier of frontier models as the other agents; this is vendor-reported. 02: LoRA rank 16 on all linear layers of Qwen3-0.6B trains 10.09M parameters; the PEFT default of q_proj and v_proj trains only 2.29M; the counts scale with the model, the 4.4x ratio is what transfers. 03: gating the extra rewards on correctness (TAG) gives pass@4 0.581, and summing all rewards gives 0.220, from Multi-Reward Hacking on Qwen3-4B. 04: in the deck's synthetic refund-fraud lab, KFold scores AUC 0.886 because customers leak across folds, and GroupKFold scores 0.602; quote the mechanism, customer leakage inflates AUC, not the exact number. 05: a pinned temperature-0 LLM judge, re-run unchanged, flips about 5% of its verdicts and about 40% on close calls, from Pinned and Still Unstable. Sixth click: the loop closes. Say: eval failures send you back to choosing the lever; this is the whole interview story in one picture, and a real iteration often goes 01, 04, 05 and skips training. Analogy: a car mechanic diagnoses (the lever), repairs (SFT or RL), replaces bad parts (data), and road-tests before handing back the keys (eval); a failed road test sends you back to diagnosis. A likely question: "Do I need to memorise all the numbers?" No; know one per chapter and know where it comes from. Hand-off: chapter 1 asks the first question of every loop, which lever to pull before you train.

RL, preference tuning
and rewards

RL optimizes exactly what the reward pays for, including the bugs.

GRPO learns from a scored group of answers to one prompt. DPO learns from chosen / rejected reply pairs.

This chapter follows one scenario: GRPO on insurance-claim payouts ("payout" means this claims-payout task) with a boxed-answer verifier (lab2_grpo_claims.py), a correctness-gated brevity reward (lab4_rewards.py), DPO for on-brand support replies (lab6_dpo_support.py), and an RL loop on live traffic. The animation is the whole of GRPO in one picture: one claims-payout prompt gets G = 8 sampled answers, the verifier gives each a reward of 1 or 0, and here 3 of 8 are correct, so the group mean is 0.375 and the std is 0.484. The advantage is (r minus the mean) divided by the std: +1.29 for the three correct answers, which are pushed up, and -0.77 for the five wrong ones, which are pushed down. No critic model is needed, because the group itself is the baseline. Analogy: a teacher who grades on the curve of one exam room, not against an answer key of expected scores. Likely question: "What if all 8 answers get the same reward?" Then every advantage is zero and the prompt teaches nothing, which is the first question on the next slide.

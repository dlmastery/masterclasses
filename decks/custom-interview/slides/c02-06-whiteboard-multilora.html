Read the prompt and let the candidate ask questions first: how many tenants, how often adapters change, and what isolation means. Use the refund assistant as tenant A. The key insight is that 200 tenants need 200 small adapters on one shared base, not 200 model copies: 10.09M parameters are about 40 MB in fp32 or 20 MB in bf16, so 200 adapters are about 8 GB of storage, against about 240 GB for 200 bf16 copies of the 1.2 GB base. The numbered badges match the steps on the left. Steps 2 and 3 separate senior answers: a key-diff load check, a golden-output test, and a same-weights A/B with disable_adapter. Step 4 is multi-LoRA serving in vLLM. max_lora_rank must be at least the adapter rank, 16 here. max_loras is how many adapters can be in one batch on the GPU, and its default is 1; max_cpu_loras is the CPU cache. When tenant 151 calls and its adapter is not on the GPU, vLLM swaps it in from the CPU cache, and that first request pays the load cost. Weekly adapters load at runtime through /v1/load_lora_adapter when VLLM_ALLOW_RUNTIME_LORA_UPDATING is set, or with a rolling restart. Each request names its adapter in the OpenAI model field. Isolation: the router takes tenant_id from authentication, never from the request body; log the adapter name on every request, and test the tenant-to-adapter map in CI. The last step shows what fails silently: PeftModel.from_pretrained drops unexpected keys and strict=True does nothing (PEFT issue 3804), and transformers v5 module renames can make vLLM ignore an adapter (filed on the PEFT repo as issue 3896). The analogy: one engine with 200 swappable keys. Next chapter: reinforcement learning and rewards.

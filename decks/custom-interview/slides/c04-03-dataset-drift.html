Ask about coverage and provenance, not the row count

“We have 50k support-ticket rows, ready to train.” What do you ask?
Ask what the rows cover. Rows per taxonomy leaf (the lowest-level category), with a minimum n per eval slice. Provenance, license and synthetic share per subset.
KS flags days_since_order every day, but PSI says stable. Why?
At n = 20k vs 5k, a p-value flags tiny shifts. PSI is a binned distribution distance: < 0.1 stable, 0.1–0.25 moderate, > 0.25 major. Split September in two and alert above that PSI noise floor.
cleanlab flags 223 of 3,000 tickets as mislabeled. Delete them?
No: review them. pred_probs must be out-of-sample, or the model has memorized the noisy labels. In a Google Play study (870k apps), removing apps that three noise detectors flagged did not help. Relabel, retrain, re-score a frozen eval slice.

Ask each question, wait for an answer, then reveal; each answer adds one evidence card on the right, and the cards stay. Question 1 tests whether the candidate audits a dataset like a reviewer: a row count says nothing about an empty taxonomy leaf, such as eu-west outages with zero rows, an unknown license or a synthetic share. A taxonomy leaf is the lowest-level category. Question 2 is about scale: at 20,000 against 5,000 rows, KS gives p = 0.02 for days_since_order while PSI is 0.003, so the KS alert is noise. refund_amount_usd is different: PSI 0.243 and a tiny KS p-value agree, and that is real drift after the holiday promo. Set the alert threshold from a reference-versus-reference split: compute PSI between two halves of September, and alert only above that noise floor. Question 3 tests the cleanlab workflow: out-of-sample pred_probs, then a review queue, never an auto-delete list. In the Google Play study the label was a proxy, like a rushed vendor's ticket label, and deleting flagged rows did not improve the model. After relabelling, retrain and re-score a frozen eval slice. The tickets come from the lab's tickets.py generator; model is a TF-IDF plus logistic-regression pipeline. Likely follow-up: "Why must pred_probs be out-of-sample?" An in-sample model has memorized the noisy labels, so it agrees with them and cleanlab finds almost nothing. Hand-off: the next slide asks where the rows came from, which no detector can answer after training.

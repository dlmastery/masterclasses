Databricks Genie Code: quality and cost
Ask Q1 and wait. The model answer: context beats training when the gap is missing context, not missing skill. The evidence comes from a different domain, so say so: Genie Code is a Databricks data-engineering agent, not a refund assistant, and the numbers are vendor-reported on 401 internal data-agent tasks. Genie Code scored 76.6% at $0.55 per task; the best general coding agent scored 72.1% at $1.09. Read each chart on its own axis: half the cost, and higher accuracy. Click 2: the gain came from context, catalog search and persistent table memory, with 8.3 tool calls per task, and no fine-tuning. Click 3 is the bridge back to our scenario: the same holds for the refund bot. Give it a policy lookup tool that returns the clause and its version, re-run the 20-50 evals, and train only if they still fail. Analogy: before you send the clerk on a course, give the clerk the binder. Likely follow-up: "When does context stop being enough?" When the eval still fails with the right facts in context, the gap is skill or behavior, and the training ladder on the later slides applies.

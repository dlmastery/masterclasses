Plain LoRA SFT overwrites what RL built. Always run a reasoning eval.

Post-RL Qwen2.5 models: reasoning after fine-tuning

dashed = post-RL level · the two panels use different metrics
NB-LoRA: the update is constrained to the null space of the reasoning activations
Reveal one question at a time, and let the candidate answer before you show the answer. Question 1: a strong answer does the arithmetic. Qwen3-0.6B has hidden size 1024; q_proj maps 1024 to 2048, k and v map 1024 to 1024, o maps 2048 to 1024, and the MLP is 3072 wide. So q_proj adds 16 times 3072, which is 49,152; one layer adds 360,448; 28 layers give 10,092,544. PEFT's qwen3 default trains only q_proj and v_proj: 2,293,760. Why does LoRA need about 10 times the full fine-tuning learning rate? The update is B times A scaled by lora_alpha over r; keep alpha over r fixed, or set use_rslora, when you sweep r, so the effective step size does not change. Question 2: full fine-tuning with AdamW in fp32 needs 16 bytes per parameter, so Qwen3-4B needs about 64 GB before activations. QLoRA keeps the base frozen in 4-bit, about 2 GB, and trains only the 33.0M adapter parameters. NF4 places its 16 levels for normally distributed weights, which is why it beats fp4; BitsAndBytesConfig defaults to fp4, while the TRL CLI defaults to nf4. A follow-up: QLoRA saves memory, not time; AQLoRA (arXiv 2608.23816) keeps the layers with the highest NF4 error in fp16 and trains about 11% faster. Analogy: full fine-tuning keeps four copies of the library; QLoRA keeps one compressed copy and writes notes in a small notebook. Question 3: the numbers come from Qwen2.5 post-RL checkpoints in the NB-LoRA paper (arXiv 2609.25618). Plain LoRA dropped the RL reasoning score from 63.8% to 8.3% (3B) and from 80.1% to 9.4% (1.5B). NB-LoRA constrains the update to the null space of the reasoning activations, so reasoning stays near the post-RL level: 67.1% vs 67.7% and 54.3% vs 54.5% on the 5-benchmark average. Note that the two panels use different metrics. Also select checkpoints on a generation metric, not on NLL, the negative log-likelihood training loss: generation-based selection beat NLL selection by 0.71 to 0.85 points (arXiv 2609.36569).

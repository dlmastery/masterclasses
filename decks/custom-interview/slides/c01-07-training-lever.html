The base model's pass rate picks SFT, RFT or distillation

SFT or reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) for claim payouts?
Probe the base model first. RFT learns only from rollouts that pass, so it needs pass@8 > 0. Start with 100-200 examples. Reward = the grader's score in [0, 1]. pass@k uses the same grader. GRPO is the RL algorithm Bedrock RFT uses.
A strong teacher model exists. RL or distillation?
Try on-policy distillation first: the student writes, the teacher scores each student token. It costs about 10x less compute. Needs the teacher's per-token logprobs: an open-weight teacher with the same tokenizer.

First the words: the reward is the grader's score between 0 and 1, and pass@k is the share of prompts where at least 1 of k sampled answers gets full marks from the same grader. Click 1: RFT learns only from contrast between rollouts. If the base model never gets a payout right, pass@8 is 0, every reward is 0 and there is nothing to reinforce, so SFT comes first. Between 0 and 95%, RFT with GRPO has signal. Above 95% pass@1, AWS says RFT might be unnecessary, so ship or move on. During training, a mean reward that plateaus below 0.15 is the AWS red flag. Click 2: when a strong teacher exists, try on-policy distillation. The student generates, and the teacher scores every token of the student's text, so the signal is dense. The Qwen3 report figures, quoted in the Thinking Machines post, are 74.4% on AIME'24, a math contest benchmark, at 1,800 GPU hours against 67.6% at 17,920 for RL. The catch: you need the teacher's per-token logprobs on the student's tokens, which in practice means an open-weight teacher with a compatible tokenizer, not a closed API. A recent paper, arXiv 2610.18011, adds that distillation on failure-only prompts declined in all 8 configurations, so keep prompts where the student is sometimes right. Analogy: RL is a coach who says only "win" or "lose"; distillation is a coach who comments on every move. Likely follow-up: "Can I distill from a closed model?" Only off-policy, through its text, which is plain SFT on its outputs. Next: what survives when the vendor retires your tuned model.

01

Pick the lever first

Classify the failure first.

This chapter follows two assistants, an insurance-claims assistant and a refund assistant, and drills the first decision in every customization interview: which lever do you pull? Start with only the failure from a real trace. Click 1: a wrong refund clause is knowledge, so it goes to retrieval, where it stays fresh, cited and permissioned. Click 2: a reply that sounds too formal is behavior, so you start with the cheapest fix, the prompt. Click 3: you climb to SFT (supervised fine-tuning) or DPO (direct preference optimization) only when the eval still fails after the prompt fix. For goals a program can check, like a payout amount, the base model's pass rate on a probe decides between SFT and RFT (reinforcement fine-tuning). pass@k is the share of prompts where at least 1 of k sampled answers is correct. If pass@8 is 0, there is nothing for RFT to reinforce, so SFT comes first; if pass@1 is already above 95%, RFT adds little. We prove this rule on the training-lever slide and debug a broken probe on the last slide. Analogy: you do not send an employee to a training course because the policy binder is out of date. Likely question: "What if the failure is both?" Then it is two failures with two fixes, which is the first question on the next slide.

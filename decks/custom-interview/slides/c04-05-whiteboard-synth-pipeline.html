Read the prompt aloud and let the candidate draw before you reveal anything. The corpus is the HR-policy corpus from the relevance-rubric question, and the decontamination stage reuses the candidates-then-judge policy from the previous slide. The strong answer is the Databricks KARL pipeline from tech report section 4.1: a synthesis agent explores the corpus with vector search, a dedup agent decontaminates against the eval and few-shot data, solver agents attempt each question, and the pass-rate filter drops questions that every solver or no solver gets right, because both give zero learning signal. A quality-filter agent removes ambiguous items and wrong reference answers, then off-policy RL trains the agent, and the improved agent synthesizes the next round. Analogy: it is an exam committee that writes questions from the textbook, removes the ones copied from the final exam, and throws out questions that every student or no student can answer. The numbers to know: KARLBench 52.6 for the GLM 4.5 Air base, 58.9 after KARL, and 67.5 with 10 parallel traces, equal to Claude 4.6 Opus at about 33% lower cost; the token counts in the animation are an illustrative batch. Likely follow-up: "Why not keep the never-solved items as hard examples?" Many are broken, so review them, and never decontaminate with a cosine threshold alone: at 0.80, 88% of sampled removals were false (arXiv 2610.21229). Hand-off: the debug drill shows the same leak in a split: one unit in both train and test.

Read the prompt aloud and let the candidate split it before you build anything. Click 1: monthly policies are knowledge, so they go to retrieval: a versioned policy store, an access filter by product line and role so an agent sees only its own lines, hybrid search and a reranker that keeps a small top-n. Click 2: brand tone is behavior, so it goes to a small SFT or DPO adapter trained on approved replies. Click 3: the generator's citation is checked in code: the quoted text must appear in the cited clause, and on a miss you regenerate once, then refuse. Click 4: the evaluation loop. A golden set from real traces with one slice per product line, an LLM judge whose agreement with adjusters you measure on a labelled sample, and both feed a scorecard. Click 5: operate. Drift is the share of live queries whose best match in the corpus is below a similarity threshold; when it rises, the corpus no longer covers the questions. Click 6: the two follow-ups strong interviewers ask. What happens the day after a policy update, and what if the quote matches but the clause belongs to another product line. Analogy: the policy store is the rule book on the desk; the adapter is the house style the agent learned in training. Likely question: "why not fine-tune the policies in?" They change monthly, so each change would need a new training run and a new eval. Next: before the team trains anything for claim payouts, it probes the base model, and the probe has a bug.

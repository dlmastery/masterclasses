Dynamic RAG retrieves during generation: IRCoT, FLARE, DRAGIN and DRAG decide when and what to fetch

Paper IRCoT (arXiv 2212.10509) Paper FLARE (arXiv 2305.06983) Paper DRAGIN (ACL 2024) Repo oneal2000/DRAGIN Paper DRAG / IterDRAG (arXiv 2410.04343)

IRCoT Trivedi et al., ACL 2023
Each new chain-of-thought sentence becomes the next query. Up to +21 points retrieval and +15 points QA on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue and IIRC.

FLARE Jiang et al., EMNLP 2023
Draft the next sentence. If a token has low confidence, use the draft as the query and regenerate the sentence.

DRAGIN Su et al., ACL 2024
RIND decides when: entropy × attention from later tokens × stopword filter > θ. QFS decides what: the tokens in the whole context that the trigger token attends to most. It needs logits and attention, so it cannot run on a closed API.

DRAG / IterDRAG Yue et al., Google DeepMind, ICLR 2025
DRAG adds many documents and in-context demos. IterDRAG splits the question into sub-queries with interleaved retrieval (max 5 iterations). Up to 58.9% over standard RAG: spend inference compute on retrieval.

Acronym trap: “DRAG” names at least four other papers. Always cite the arXiv ID, not the acronym.
Classic RAG retrieves once before generation. Dynamic RAG retrieves while the model writes. IRCoT uses each new chain-of-thought sentence as the next query, which gave up to 21 points better retrieval and 15 points better QA on four multi-hop sets. FLARE drafts the next sentence and retrieves only when a token has low confidence; the red token is the trigger. DRAGIN makes both decisions from the model's internals: RIND fires on an uncertain, important, non-stopword token, and QFS builds the query from the context tokens that this token attends to, not only from the last sentence. That is why DRAGIN needs logits and attention weights, which hosted APIs do not return. DRAG and IterDRAG from Google DeepMind show that RAG accuracy grows nearly linearly with effective context when you allocate compute well: up to 58.9 percent over standard RAG. Analogy: a student who checks the textbook each time she is unsure, not once at the start. Likely question: "Which of these works with Gemini or GPT through an API?" IRCoT and IterDRAG yes, FLARE only if the API returns token log-probabilities, DRAGIN no.

arXiv 2610.30612 2026-10-22

The Fusion Tax: A Diagnostic Analysis of When Hybrid Retrieval Hurts

On 7 BEIR corpora the best fusion still ranks relevant documents below dense-only on 8-21% of queries. RRF is worse than dense-only on average on 5 of 7 corpora and wins only where both retrievers are balanced. A learned per-query router never beat static interpolation on held-out corpora.
Label 200-400 real queries. Count the four quadrants. Pick one static policy per corpus: interpolation α ≈ 0.6-0.7 when dense leads, RRF when balanced, or dense-only. Re-check when the corpus or encoder changes.

This slide is the counterweight to the four steps of Lab 4: hybrid search wins on average, and you still have to check it query by query. The Fusion Tax paper sorts every query into four quadrants by which retriever finds a relevant document in its top 10; even the best fusion ranks relevant documents below dense-only on 8 to 21 percent of queries, and plain RRF is worse than dense-only on average on 5 of 7 BEIR corpora. The dots here are schematic, not the paper's counts. The Thomson Reuters paper shows that good fusion weights form an interval for each query, so one tuned static weight is the right first step, and their code is public. The clinical-notes study is the reranking version of the same lesson: Hit@10 rose 14 points, answer correctness rose under 4. Analogy: a team average can rise while two players get worse; you only see it in the per-player stats. Likely question: "So should I turn hybrid off?" No; measure it on 200 to 400 labelled queries from your own traffic and pick the policy per corpus.

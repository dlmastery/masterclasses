Chapter 10 is Lab 9 at the Harbor Mutual claims desk: ClaimsKB holds a public FAQ, adjuster manuals and the SIU fraud playbook, and call-centre agent Maria must never see the playbook. The loop shows the three gates we build: the ACL filter inside the vector query stops the SIU card, datamarking marks the retrieved text as data so the KB page with hidden instructions stays inert, and a deterministic egress allowlist checks every URL in the answer. Analogy: a hospital pharmacy checks who you are before it opens the cabinet, labels every bottle, and checks the bag again at the door. Then we look at 2026 evidence: injections work much better through retrieved documents than through chat, coordinated poison passes ingestion checks, and citations can point to the wrong source. Likely question: "Is a prompt-injection classifier enough?" No: the papers later in this chapter show that detectors miss fluent false facts, so we test outcomes, not detections.

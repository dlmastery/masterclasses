Ingestion and chunking

Retrieval quality is decided at ingestion, before the first query. Tables, columns, scans and charts break naive text extraction.

Three questions for this chapter

  1. Parse. How do you read layout, tables and reading order?
  2. Chunk. Where do you cut the text?
  3. Contextualize. How does each chunk keep its context?

Open the chapter with the claim that most retrieval failures start at ingestion, not at query time. Step 1 splits the page into its real blocks: title, two text columns, a table and a chart. A naive extractor does not see these blocks. Step 2 turns each block into a chunk card with a context tag, which is the target shape for the whole chapter. Analogy: a librarian who cuts a book into index cards must write the book title and chapter on every card, or the cards are useless later. Likely question: "Is parsing really worth the effort if the embedder is good?" Answer: the embedder can only encode the text it gets; scrambled columns stay scrambled.

import math
HALF_LIFE_DAYS = 365
LAM = math.log(2) / HALF_LIFE_DAYS
def decay_weight(age_days: float) -> float:
    return math.exp(-LAM * age_days)
print(round(decay_weight(365), 2))   # 0.5

Blog Building Uber's redlining agent (2026-10-08)
Uber Legal's redlining agent, published on 8 October 2026, is a production example of memory built from past decisions. Steps 1 and 2: a Word add-in sends each contract sentence verbatim to a LangGraph backend, where intent detection, risk assessment and policy lookup run in parallel; OpenSearch with nomic-embed-text-v1.5 and metadata filters narrows to about 20 candidates, a second LLM pass checks intent, and then adjudicate, tone and deterministic rules run in order. Step 3: every past lawyer decision gets the weight exp(-lambda times age in days) with a 365-day half-life, so a one-year-old decision counts half, and agree and disagree examples are kept balanced. Step 4: Uber reports more than 20% less average review time and 91% decision accuracy, but does not describe how it measured the accuracy. Analogy: a senior lawyer still remembers last year's call, but trusts last month's call more. Likely question: "Why not just delete old decisions?" Because a decay keeps rare cases findable, while recent practice still wins ties. Tie back to Harbor Mutual: the raw turns in case_memory.py carry a date, so recall() can weight each hit by decay_weight() of its age.

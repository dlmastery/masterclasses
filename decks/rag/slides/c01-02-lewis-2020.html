The query encoder makes q(x). MIPS returns the top-k passages z. BART writes the answer y token by token.

RAG-Sequence. One passage conditions the whole answer: p(y|x) ≈ Σz p(z|x) · Πi p(yi|x, z, y<i)

RAG-Token. Each token can use a different passage: p(y|x) ≈ Πi Σz p(z|x) · p(yi|x, z, y<i). Both marginalize over the top-k.

Paper Lewis et al., arXiv 2005.11401 (NeurIPS 2020)
This is the paper that named RAG: Lewis et al., FAIR, UCL and NYU, NeurIPS 2020. Step 1 draws the pipeline: query encoder, MIPS over 21M passages, top-k passages, BART generator. Steps 2 and 3 show the two variants: RAG-Sequence keeps one passage for the whole answer, RAG-Token lets each token use a different passage; both sum over the top-k. Step 4 is the headline: a 400M generator with an index gets 44.5 exact match on Natural Questions, against 34.5 for closed-book T5-11B, a model about 27 times larger. Step 5 is the experiment to remember: swap the 2016 index for the 2018 index and the answers change with it, no retraining. Analogy: the weights are what you remember, the index is your notebook. Likely question: "Is the retriever trained end to end?" Only the query encoder is fine-tuned; the document encoder and the index stay frozen, so the index never needs a rebuild during training.

Setup and API keys: two slides ahead.

Click 1: one real support question from the Northwind Outfitters corpus in kb.py, q04, "Can I return a monogrammed backpack?". A closed-book model guesses "Yes, within 30 days" because most return policies say that. Click 2: retrieval sends the question into the chunk index; a chunk is a small piece of a policy document, and the three nearest chunks come back: c03, c09 and c01. Click 3: check means "keep only the chunks that answer the question"; c09 is the backpack warranty and c01 is the general return window, so only c03 stays. Later chapters also check that the final answer is supported by the chunk before we reply. Click 4: the model answers "No" and cites c03, which says personalised (monogrammed) products cannot be returned. Click 5: we measure the ranked list, not the answer: R@3 asks whether the right chunk is in the top 3, and MRR asks how high it is; Lab 1 builds both. Click 6: why measure every change? A paper from October 2026 stacked the three best single upgrades; their solo gains predicted +47% nDCG@10, but the stack gave +17%. Analogy: RAG is an open-book exam with a strict grader. Likely question: "Is RAG dead with 1M-token windows?" No; chapter 5 tests that directly. The next slide maps the ten labs.

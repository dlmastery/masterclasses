# kb.py -- shared corpus + labelled queries (gold chunk ids) for all labs
DOCS = {
    "D1": "Returns & Refunds Policy (US), v2026-09",
    "D2": "Warranty Policy: Tents and Backpacks",
    "D3": "Payments Troubleshooting Runbook for Support Agents",
    "D4": "Shipping & Delivery FAQ",
}
CHUNKS = [
    ("c01", "D1", "Northwind Outfitters accepts returns of unworn items within 30 days of delivery. "
                  "Items must have original tags attached."),
    ("c02", "D1", "Refunds are issued to the original payment method within 5-7 business days "
                  "after the warehouse scans the return."),
    ("c03", "D1", "Final-sale items, gift cards and personalised (monogrammed) products cannot be returned."),
    ("c04", "D1", "Members of the Trailhead loyalty programme get an extended window of 60 days."),
    # c05-c06, c08-c10 omitted on the slide: damaged parcels, tent and backpack warranty
    ("c07", "D2", "The window starts on the purchase date, not the delivery date. "
                  "Proof of purchase is required."),
    ("c11", "D3", "Error E-4012 means the card issuer declined the payment. "
                  "Ask the customer to contact their bank or use another card."),
    # c12-c18 omitted on the slide: 3-D Secure timeout, P-220 gift card, shipping, duties
]
QUERIES = {  # query_id: (text, set of relevant chunk ids)
    "q01": ("how long do I have to send back a jacket I haven't worn?", {"c01"}),
    "q02": ("customer sees E-4012 at checkout", {"c11"}),
    "q11": ("loyalty member: how long is the return window and when is the refund paid?", {"c04", "c02"}),
    "q15": ("P-220", {"c13"}),
    # q03-q10, q12-q14 omitted on the slide
}
IDS = [c[0] for c in CHUNKS]
TEXT = {c[0]: c[2] for c in CHUNKS}
DOC_OF = {c[0]: c[1] for c in CHUNKS}
def doc_text(doc_id: str) -> str:
    return DOCS[doc_id] + "\n\n" + "\n".join(t for _, d, t in CHUNKS if d == doc_id)

Keep chunks as a naive splitter leaves them. c07 never says tent or warranty.

Mix paraphrases (q01), exact codes (q02, q15) and a two-part question: q11 has two gold chunks.

IDS, TEXT, DOC_OF map ids to text. doc_text() rebuilds one parent document.

15 queries show the method only. One flipped query moves recall@5 by 6.7 points. Decide with 100-300 real ticket queries.

This is the run-it-Monday slide for Lab 1: make a Python 3.12 venv, pin numpy 2.5.4, bm25s 0.3.15 and PyStemmer 3.1.0, and write kb.py before any retriever exists. The slide shows 7 of the 18 chunks and 4 of the 15 queries; the full file is in the lab handout. Walk the three focus bands: the chunks stay as a naive splitter leaves them (c07 never names the tent), the queries mix paraphrases, exact error codes and one two-part question with two gold chunks, and the helpers map ids to text and rebuild a parent document. Analogy: the labelled set is the answer key you write before you grade any exam. Likely question: "Are 15 queries enough?" No: one flipped query moves recall@5 by 6.7 points, so decisions need 100-300 labelled queries from real support tickets, labelled at the evidence-span level.

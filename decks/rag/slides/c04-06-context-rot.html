More context is not better context: models miss facts in the middle and degrade with length

Lost in the Middle (Liu et al., TACL 2024): accuracy is highest when the relevant document is at the start or the end. In the middle, GPT-3.5-Turbo fell below its closed-book score. More documents made it worse than none.

Context Rot (Chroma, 14 Jul 2025, 18 models incl. Claude 4, GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5, Qwen3): models "do not use their context uniformly". "Even a single distractor reduces performance." Shuffled haystacks beat coherent ones. Note: Chroma sells retrieval infrastructure. Kelly Hong: "Simply having the right information in the context is not enough."

  • Rerank, then send fewer chunks.
  • Put the best chunks first and last (LangChain LongContextReorder).
  • Remove near-duplicates before you build the prompt.
Raising top-k to 50 "to be safe" adds distractors and pushes the answer into the middle.

First the axes: the x-axis is where the relevant document sits among 20 documents. The curve is U-shaped: the model reads the start and the end well and the middle badly; I draw the shape only, the paper has the exact values. Then the dashed line: GPT-3.5-Turbo with no documents at all scored 56.1%, and with 20 or 30 documents and the answer in the middle it scored 53.8% and 50.5%, so the red area is where retrieval made the answer worse. Chroma's Context Rot report adds that a focused 300-token prompt beat the full 113k-token history in every model family, and that even one distractor hurts. Analogy: a long meeting where the key decision was made in minute 40 of 90; everyone remembers the opening and the close. Likely question: "Do 1M-token models fix this?" No; the report tested 2025 frontier models and still saw non-uniform use of the context, so rerank, send less, and put the best evidence at the edges.

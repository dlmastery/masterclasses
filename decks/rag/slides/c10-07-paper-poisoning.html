Coverage Is Not Containment

CiteShade: citation laundering in multi-source RAG

One instruction-free attacker source: wrong answers 0.01 → 0.68, cited to a trusted source that does not support them (laundering 0.84 with cite instructions, 0.64 without). Perplexity filters and support checks are each insufficient.
High-stakes answers: leave-one-source-out. Flag when the driver is not the cited source.

Stale-Document Poisoning

Outdated, authentic docs flipped 30% (Llama) / 37% (Qwen) of correct answers; 66% / 75% with "follow the document". A recency-aware reranker cut poisoning 4.6–10.0 pts when dates were accurate.

None of these three attacks contains an instruction, so datamarking and the Lab 9 injection suite do not stop them. First, Coverage Is Not Containment proves that ten individually normal documents can form a cone around one query and take all ten top-k slots, and that no ingestion-time filter can tell that cone from a real niche upload; the fix sits on the read path, where a demand monitor caught every attack at a 1 percent false-positive rate. Second, CiteShade shows that one fluent attacker source can drive a wrong answer while the citation points at a clean, trusted manual, so only a leave-one-source-out check finds the real driver. Third, your own corpus can be the attacker: an outdated but authentic manual flips a third of correct answers, so store validity dates and show them to the model. Analogy: a forged letter on real letterhead passes the mailroom; you catch it only by watching who it changes. A related ops pattern, Bounded Provisional Visibility (2610.05826), hides unvetted new items after a deadline and cut median poisoned retrievals from 34 to 7. Likely question: "Which do I build first?" Validity metadata, because it is cheap and also fixes plain staleness. Next, the whole chapter as one checklist of testable rules.

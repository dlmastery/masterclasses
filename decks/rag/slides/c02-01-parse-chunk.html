Parse and chunk: retrieval quality is decided at ingestion

Bad parsing and bad boundaries cannot be fixed by a better retriever.

Docling 2.142.1 · tables + pages HybridChunker · embedder tokens Unstructured 0.27.34 · chunk_by_title langchain-text-splitters 1.1.3 out/claims_chunks.jsonl
This chapter is Lab 2: document intake for Northwind Mutual, a fictional insurer. Docling converts the claims-policy PDF and keeps tables and page numbers, HybridChunker sizes the chunks in the embedding model's own tokens, Unstructured chunks the HR handbook by title, and LangChain splitters show how naive 200-character windows cut policy rules away from their headings. The animation is the whole chapter in one picture: a page becomes typed layout boxes, the boxes become chunk cards, and each card keeps its heading path and its page so an answer can cite "page 4, 3.2 Exclusions". Analogy: parsing is the kitchen prep; if the onions arrive in the soup with the skins on, a better waiter does not fix the dish. Likely question: "Why not just tune the retriever?" Because the retriever can only rank the chunks you give it; a split table or an orphaned rule is lost before ranking starts. Every lab here is pinned: docling 2.142.1, unstructured 0.27.34, langchain-text-splitters 1.1.3.

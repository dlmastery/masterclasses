arXiv 2610.28340 20 Oct 2026 · Purdue

DeclineCraft: A RAG Abstention Benchmark

27 LLMs. Iterative RAG (self-assess + up to 2 more retrievals) lowered correct abstention in 30 of 40 configurations. Accuracy barely moved: 68.5% → 69.3%. Rewriting + reranking on hybrid retrieval lowered it for all 12 models tested. Model size predicts accuracy, not abstention.
Track the correct-abstention rate whenever you add agentic retrieval.
arXiv 2609.23053 19 Sep 2026

Attributable Post-Rationalization in RAG Citations

Plant the claim's longest 4-gram into an uncited passage and re-run. About 1 in 7 citations moves to the planted passage. RLVR-trained agents are no better than the base model.
Report a citation-move rate next to answer accuracy. It needs no labels and 2 generations per claim.

These three papers change what the ShopCo gate measures, not how the bot answers. The canary paper shows why accuracy alone is a trap: systems that all looked 97 to 98 percent accurate when they answered differed roughly sixfold on questions they should have refused, so we score wrong answers at minus 4 and give canaries zero tolerance. DeclineCraft warns that the features we added in earlier chapters, rewriting, reranking and iterative retrieval, made models abstain less on unanswerable questions while accuracy stayed flat, so the correct-abstention rate must sit on the same dashboard. The swap test needs no labels: plant the claim's longest 4-gram in an uncited passage, re-run, and see if the citation follows the words; about one citation in seven does. Analogy: a student who cites whichever book has the matching sentence, not the book they read. A likely question is "does RL training fix it?": in this study RLVR agents post-rationalized at the base model's rate. Next: how Shopify learned to trust its own judge in production.

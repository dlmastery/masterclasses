Evaluation and security

A RAG system is as good as its measured retrieval and as safe as its least trusted document.

  • Fails in three places: retrieval, grounding, usefulness. Score each one separately.
  • Is attacked in three places: the documents, the index, the tools.

“LLM-Integrated Applications blur the line between data and instructions.”

Paper Greshake et al. 2023, Not what you've signed up for
This chapter answers two questions: how do you know the system works, and how do you stop it from being turned against its users. Step 1: three failure points pulse. Retrieval returns the wrong chunks, the model writes claims the context does not support, or the answer is grounded but does not answer the question. Step 2: three attack points appear. Attackers write into documents, read across the index without permission, and use tools to send data out. Step 3: the Greshake quote is the root cause: the model reads retrieved text and instructions in one token stream. Analogy: a new employee who obeys every sticky note in the filing cabinet. Likely question: "Is security not a separate team's job?" Answer: no, because the retriever decides what text the model reads, so the RAG engineer owns this boundary.

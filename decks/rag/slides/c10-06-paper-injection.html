The same injection succeeds 18% of the time as a chat follow-up and 53% inside retrieved context. Channel-aware training cut RAG-framed attack success 65.5% → 6.1% (Qwen2.5-3B, GSM8K) and kept 78–82% clean accuracy.

arXiv 2610.19133 · TRACER arXiv 2610.28364 · nokast-secureRAG arXiv 2610.08571 · RAG-PIBench arXiv 2608.17153 · detection ≠ resistance
Three recent papers explain why the Lab 9 suite tests both vectors and asserts outcomes. TRACER shows that the channel matters: the same injection succeeds 18 percent of the time as a chat message but 53 percent inside a retrieved document, and channel-aware training brought RAG-framed attack success from 65.5 to 6.1 percent on a small Qwen model. nokast-secureRAG and RAG-PIBench show that detectors are a good cheap first gate, but a 0.994-recall firewall falls to near zero on PoisonedRAG, because a fluent false fact contains no instruction to detect. The third paper shows that a model can detect poison and still be steered by it: reasoning cut leakage but raised attack success. Analogy: a metal detector finds knives, not forged documents. Likely question: "Should I fine-tune like TRACER?" Only if you train your own model; everyone else should red-team the retrieval channel and keep the outcome tests. Next: attacks that carry no instruction at all, so neither a detector nor datamarking can see them.

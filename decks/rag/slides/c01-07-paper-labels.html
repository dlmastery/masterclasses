How Ground Truth Shapes Retrieval Scores: Evidence-Aware Evaluation of a Retrieval-Augmented Generation System for Breast Lesions

Curated evidence-span labels raised accepted chunk targets from 36 to 51 (spans 25 → 37). Retrieval rankings did not change. Best setup: 384-token chunks, 20% overlap, hybrid + cross-encoder, R@3 = MRR@3 = 0.94 on 18 queries.
Label every chunk that holds the answer, overlapping chunks too, or a good retriever looks bad.

Two October papers say the same thing from two sides: your score depends on your labels and on which metric you print. Step 1 and 2: in the breast-lesion RAG study, curating evidence-span labels raised the accepted chunk targets from 36 to 51 and the spans from 25 to 37, and the ranking did not change at all; only the measured score did. Analogy: a teacher with an incomplete answer key marks correct answers wrong. Step 3: the Bottleneck paper shows that similarity search finds some relevant chunk far more often than all required chunks, so hit@k flatters multi-fact questions; our q11 needs both c04 and c02. A likely question is "Which number do I report?": print recall@k over all gold next to hit@k, and keep source and title in every chunk's metadata. Next: the chapter's rules in one checklist.

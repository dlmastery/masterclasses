Embeddings and vector indexes

An encoder maps meaning to a vector. Similar meanings land near each other. An index finds the nearest vectors fast.

Similarity is geometry

Use cosine, dot product on normalized vectors, or L2. In 2026 one space can hold text, images, audio, video and PDFs.

Exact search does not scale

Exact k-NN costs O(N × d) per query. ANN gives up a little recall for orders of magnitude more speed.

Three questions for this chapter

Which model?How many dimensions?Which index?
This chapter is about the geometry under every dense retriever. First, the points are scattered; press next and they move into topic clusters, because the encoder puts similar meanings near each other. Then the grid shows how an index cuts the space into cells, so a query only looks in a few cells and not at every vector. Analogy: a library puts books on shelves by subject, so you walk to one shelf and do not read every spine. A student often asks: "Why not just compute exact distances?" Answer: exact k-NN is O(N × d) per query, which is too slow at tens of millions of vectors, and ANN keeps most of the recall at a small fraction of the cost.

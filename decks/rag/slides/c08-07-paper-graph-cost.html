LLM-free traversal with query-adaptive thresholds and community-aware hub penalties, sent as short reasoning chains. >100× lower latency and >99% lower cost than GraphRAG Global/DRIFT. It matches LinearRAG with about 14× fewer tokens.

These three papers tell you where the money goes in graph and multimodal RAG. EffiRAG says that the graph is good at finding which passages matter, but the answer must come from the original text; on 93 of 120 UltraDomain questions that beat LightRAG-hybrid at 57 percent lower cost, which is why the lab used mix mode and file paths. LiteRAG removes the LLM from traversal: it penalises hub nodes, like "Harbor Mutual" here, that connect everything, and sends a short chain of relations, for over 99 percent lower cost than GraphRAG Global. Loud and Clear moves to images: the generator often reads a retrieved page at too low a resolution, and a 32K page budget plus the OCR text of matched regions fixes much of that. Analogy: a library catalogue tells you which shelf to visit, but you quote the book, not the catalogue card. Also mention the Commercial Tax paper, 2608.16096: GraphRAG indexing for one 5.64 MB corpus cost USD 2.30 to 24.94, and the NV-Embed-v2 model behind top multi-hop scores is CC-BY-NC, so check cost and licence before you copy a recipe. Likely question: "Is GraphRAG dead?" No; use it to locate evidence, measure its indexing cost, and answer from the source chunks.

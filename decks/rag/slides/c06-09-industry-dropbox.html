Dropbox Dash is a work search and chat product over many third-party apps, and its team measured what our IT branch is about to face. Step 1 is the race: an agent with one MCP tool per app needed up to 45 seconds for a simple query, while the same question against Dash's own index came back within seconds; the timeline on the slide is schematic, only the 45 seconds is their number. Step 2 shows why the index wins: connectors crawl apps and their ACLs, content becomes markdown with metadata and embeddings, the knowledge graph ships as bundles through the normal index, and a hybrid BM25 plus dense store with multi-pass ranking sits behind one super tool that replaced 5 to 10 retrieval tools. Step 3: they treat the judge as a product, starting at 8 percent disagreement with humans and lowering it with explanations, o3, RAG as a judge and DSPy. Analogy: a library catalogue beats phoning every bookshop in town. Likely question: "So is MCP bad?" No, the Dash MCP server itself exposes one tool; the lesson is fewer, better retrieval tools, which is why our Lab 5 agent searches the KB first and keeps web search in one sub-agent.

Three recent papers tell you what changes after day one, when the corpus and the models move. Step 1: a controlled study re-embedded half of an index with a new model and kept half on the old one; the mixed index recovered only 4.96 of the 11.90 points that a full re-embed gives, and retrieval caused 81% of the loss, so build the new version side by side and switch in one step. Step 2: graph search does not scale like log N at realistic sizes; at fixed recall the cost grows as a power of N, so the efSearch value from the Lab 3 sweep goes stale when the corpus grows 10x. Step 3: for filtered multi-tenant graphs, large merge batches make staleness worse faster than linearly, with recall@10 falling from 99.44% to 99.01% at 20x merge size, so merge small and often and keep tenants apart. Analogy: two maps of one city in different projections; you cannot draw one route across both. Likely question: "Can I re-embed only the hot documents first?" The first paper says that a half-migrated index keeps most of the old loss, so dual-read for evaluation but never serve a mixed index. Hand-off: Notion shows the same three lessons at production scale.

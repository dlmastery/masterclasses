Gemini: implicit caching is on by default for 2.5+ (minimum 2,048 / 4,096 tokens). Explicit client.caches.create has a 1 h default TTL. It is not supported on Gemini 4 Argon and later. Anthropic: up to 4 breakpoints, minimum 512–4,096 tokens by model.

  • Anthropic: if the knowledge base is "smaller than 200,000 tokens (about 500 pages)", skip RAG. Put it in the prompt with caching: >2× lower latency, up to 90% lower cost.
  • CAG (Chan et al., arXiv 2412.15605): preload the corpus and precompute the KV cache. No retriever. It works only when the corpus fits.
  • Self-Route (Google DeepMind, EMNLP 2024): long context wins on average when resourced, but RAG is far cheaper. The model checks if the retrieved context is enough. If not, it falls back to long context.
  • LaRA (ICML 2025, 2,326 cases, 11 LLMs): "no silver bullet". Llama 4 Scout claims 10M tokens, but it was trained at 256K.
  • 2026 synthesis: not RAG vs long context, but RAG into long context. Retrieve and rerank to a focused set. Use the big window for breadth and caching.

Walk the decision tree one branch at a time. If the corpus is under about 200k tokens, does not change, and every user may see all of it, put it in the prompt and cache it; Anthropic reports more than 2x lower latency and up to 90% lower cost, and CAG goes further by precomputing the KV cache. If the corpus is large, changes daily, or has per-user permissions, you need hybrid retrieval with reranking, because you cannot cache a different prompt for every user and every edit. If one retrieval pass misses facts that need several hops, move to an agentic loop or Self-Route, where the model decides if the retrieved context is enough. The cost bars show why caching pays: you pay 1.25x or 2x once to write and 0.1x on every read. Analogy: a small reference card lives on your desk; the full library needs a catalogue and a librarian. Likely question: "With 10M-token models, why retrieve at all?" Cost, latency, freshness and permissions, plus the context-rot results from the previous slide.

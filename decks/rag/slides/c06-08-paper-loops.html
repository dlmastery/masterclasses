These three papers from the last three months test the design choices we just built into the Northwind workflow. The first one shows that agentic RAG answer quality usually flattens before the loop stops by itself: stopping early cut about 11% of iterations and kept about 98% of the quality, so a hard MAX_ATTEMPTS of 3 with a grader-owned stop is a sound budget, not a shortcut. The second paper finds that retrieval can hurt answers and that a simple uncertainty or retrieval-score trigger often matches a learned router, and the winner changes from dataset to dataset; the animation swaps the two bars for that reason, so log realized usage and harm rate for each route. The third paper, from consumer banking, adds a middle outcome, "related mismatch", between answer and no answer, and with hybrid retrieval and a cross-encoder raised Top-1 from 50.48% to 79.05%, but on only 210 queries, so treat it as a strong hint, not a benchmark. Analogy: a good librarian says "we have a book about X, not Y" instead of "nothing found". Likely question: "Should we train a router?" Start with a score threshold, measure harm per route on your own traffic, and train a router only if it beats the threshold there.

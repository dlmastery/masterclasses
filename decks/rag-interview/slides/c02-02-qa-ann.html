Ask each question and give the room twenty seconds before you reveal the answer. Q1: 1B vectors × 768 dims (already MRL-truncated from 3,072) × 4 bytes per float32 is 3.07 TB, so it does not fit in RAM. Keep only compact codes in RAM: RaBitQ-SSD keeps 768/8 + 12 = 108 bytes per vector, so 108 GB at 1B. The paper itself reports 10B vectors at about 3,200 QPS and 90% recall on one server. Keep the rest on SSD, re-score the shortlist with full vectors to win back the recall that the codes lose, and prove it with recall@k against an exact IndexFlatIP on a query sample. Q2: vectors × dims × bytes, then add the HNSW links. Layer 0 stores 2·M neighbour ids per node, 128 bytes per vector at M=16; the upper layers add about 1/M more. Quantisation does not shrink the links, so at binary codes the links are bigger than the vectors. Truncated gemini-embedding-001 vectors also need normalisation (slide on filters and normalisation). Analogy: compression is a thumbnail gallery in RAM with the full photos on disk. Likely follow-up: "What recall do binary codes lose?" It depends on the data, so measure it; the re-score step with full vectors is what brings it back.

Whiteboard: a deep-research feature with a fixed token budget

Prompt

Acme Outfitters claims team: one question in, a cited report out in under 10 minutes, with a hard token budget. One misleading source must not flip the conclusion.

  1. Brief. The planner drafts a plan. The analyst edits it.
  2. Split. Retrieve per clue, union, rerank. Accuracy 83.1% → 90.5%.
  3. Capped lanes. Sum usage_metadata tokens per lane; stop at the token or time cap. Pruning saves up to 73%.
  4. Verify on entry. Without a gate, one misleading doc: false conclusions 0% → 54.7%.
  5. Compress. Notes keep source URLs. Block early conclusions until evidence is in: -59.1%.
  6. Cite raw chunks. Notes-based citations look right (0.86) but match the source only 0.12.

Read the prompt, then ask what "under 10 minutes" and "hard budget" mean for the design: the budget must live in code as per-lane token and time caps, not in a prompt. Build the answer in the order of the steps. 1: an editable brief. 2: a deterministic first move that splits the question into clues, retrieves per clue, unions and reranks (Question's Gambit took BrowseComp-Plus accuracy from 83.1% to 90.5%). 3: capped parallel lanes; each lane sums the usage_metadata token counts of its LLM calls and stops at its cap; all lanes share the search tool. Watch the meter: lane 2 hits the 50k lane cap, lane 3 is pruned early, and the request ends under the 200k hard cap; these numbers are illustrative. 4: every lane feeds one evidence bus into a gate where text enters the research state; the misleading doc bounces off, because without a gate one misleading document raised false-conclusion adoption from 0% to 54.7% (MisKnow). 5: lanes write compressed notes with source URLs; DeepRewind's commitment gate blocks a conclusion until its evidence is checked and cut premature commitments by 59.1%. 6: the writer cites raw chunks, because citations made from compressed notes scored 0.86 precision against the notes but only 0.12 against the source spans. Analogy: a newsroom gives each reporter a word limit and a fact-checker at the door, not at the printing press. Likely follow-up (last click): "How do you evaluate it?" Report a closed-book baseline and block benchmark leakage; in the Glasseek audit, one agent dropped from 63.4 to 58.3 once leaks were blocked.

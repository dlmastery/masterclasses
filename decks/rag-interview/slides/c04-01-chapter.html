This chapter tests one habit: does the candidate default to agents, or earn them? First click: the graph of the Acme Outfitters refund agent draws in. The token goes router, retrieve, generate and verify; the draft is unsupported, and the counter shows tries 1 of 2. Second click: the draft goes back to generate once (tries 2 of 2), fails again, and leaves to the fallback, a human refunds specialist. A fully supported draft leaves to publish instead. The budget lives in code, and recursion_limit=25 on invoke() is only the safety net around the whole graph. Analogy: a thermostat with a cut-out switch; the loop can run, but a hard limit stops it. Likely question: "Why not let the model decide when to stop?" Because LangGraph 1.2.17 sets the default recursion limit to 10007 (langgraph/_internal/_config.py: getenv("LANGGRAPH_DEFAULT_RECURSION_LIMIT", "10007")), so a loop with no counter can spend thousands of paid calls before it fails. The setup line is the one install for the whole chapter; LightRAG reads GEMINI_API_KEY, so export that too for the graph question.

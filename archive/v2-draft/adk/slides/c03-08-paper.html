Two October papers look at the cheapest part of a support graph to optimise: the routing decision. Step 0: in Jev-LiteMAS, routing and review take 31.7 to 43.5 percent of all generative tokens across three multi-agent frameworks, so the router is often a large part of the bill. Jev is a non-generative decision model: it returns a calibrated probability, not text. Step 1: replace the generative router with that lightweight decision model, and the total generative tokens fall to 45 to 87 percent of the baseline (a 12.7 to 54.6 percent cut), with accuracy within 1.34 points, as long as the decision reads the real expert outputs. For Lab 3 that means: keep classify on a flash model with a Literal schema, and let route_ticket decide. Step 2: the second paper warns that a cheaper, faster decision can lower the cost per attempt but raise the cost per success, because failed runs repeat work; the chart shows the direction only, not measured values. Step 3: measure cost per resolved ticket by summing the token counts in event.usage_metadata for each session that ends resolved. Analogy: a cheap triage nurse saves money only if fewer patients come back. A likely question is "Do I need the Jev model in ADK?": no, the same idea is a Literal classifier on a flash model plus a function that picks the route. Next: the chapter rules on one slide.

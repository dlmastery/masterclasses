Vercel's d0 agent answers data questions with SQL. Version 1 had 17 specialised tools; version 2 gives the model two general tools and lets it grep a well-documented semantic layer. On their 5 test queries it went from 274.8 to 77.4 seconds, from 4 of 5 to 5 of 5 correct, and from about 102k to 61k tokens; the worst old run took 724 seconds and 100 steps and still failed. Be honest about the caveat: five queries is an anecdote, and the trick only works when the data is documented well enough to search. Shopify's Sidekick team saw the same pattern and published tool-count bands: under 20 tools the boundaries are clear, from 20 to 50 they blur, and past 50 the model finds many ways to do the same task; their fix was to return instructions just in time with the tool result. Analogy: a junior with two sharp tools and a good manual beats one with a drawer of 17 gadgets. Likely question: "So should I merge the three refund desk tools?" No: keep them, and add or merge tools only when an eval shows a measured gain. Chapter 2 moves to the Northwind Insurance claims API: structured output, state and sessions.

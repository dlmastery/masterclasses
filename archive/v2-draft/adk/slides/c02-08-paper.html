Two recent papers change how we write the ClaimIntake schema. Step 0: the instruction and the output_schema both reach the model; the schema starts with the label fields. Step 1: make them conflict. The instruction says high means injury only, and the Field description says high means a loss over $10k. The paper measured accuracy drops of 5 to 45 points from conflicts like this; Claude Haiku 4.5 fell from 52.5% to 7%. Step 2: make the two texts say the same thing, and move the reasoning field to the top; a required reasoning field before the label raised schema-only accuracy by 15 to 24 points. Our Lab 2 schema already does both. Step 3: the second paper shows constrained decoding made JSON 100% valid in small models, but the meaning can still be wrong: a valid "high" with no injury and a $6,400 loss. Step 4: so check_claim runs in your code right after get_session and raises ValueError. Analogy: a form with a box for each answer still lets you write the wrong answer in the right box. A likely question: "Does output_schema not validate this already?" It validates types and enum values, not business rules, so the business rule lives in your code.

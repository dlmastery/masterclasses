This slide is the map for the whole chapter: seven patterns, ordered by cost, and the ADK 2.0 feature that builds each of them. Each click animates one cell and fills the cost meter: the router from Chapter 3, then the four shapes we build today (fan-out with a JoinNode, a loop with a routed back-edge and a round counter, an orchestrator with ctx.run_node, and AgentTool subagents), then RequestInput from Chapter 5 and the deep agent from Chapters 7 and 8. Read the grid like a menu with prices: every cell to the right adds model calls, latency or context, so you pick the cheapest one that passes your eval. The "use when" line under each cell is the decision rule. Shopify's advice from building Sidekick is "Avoid multi-agent architectures early." Classic ReAct, a reason-then-act loop inside one prompt, is only history here: ADK 2.0 puts that loop into a graph that code controls. A likely question: "Is a deep agent always better?" No: the papers later in this chapter show that every handoff can drop a fact. Lab 4 now builds cells 2 to 5 on the Northwind back office, and starts with the fan-out.

A pause longer than the TTL drops the cached prefix. The next turn re-sends the whole handbook.
TTL counts from cache creation on turn 2: 900 s ends at minute 17, 3600 s at minute 62.
Set one full pair: token_threshold + event_retention_size, or compaction_interval + overlap_size. Half a pair raises ValueError. If you set both pairs, the token pair wins for that turn.
Measure: ADK_EXPERIMENTAL_TELEMETRY_FEATURES=context_cache,compaction,token_usage adds cache and compaction counters to the ADK telemetry spans.
Post Running a Software Factory Efficiently at Uber Scale Repo apps/_configs.py · v2.13.0
The step-1 lab was tuned for a three-turn demo, but real Bluefin customers go quiet while they look for a bill or make coffee. The timeline shows one chat with turns at minutes 0, 2, 14 and 40, and the cache is created on turn 2, so each TTL counts from minute 2: a 5-minute TTL, which Uber used before, misses both long pauses, the lab's 900 seconds survives the 12-minute pause but not the 26-minute one, and 3600 seconds keeps both turns a hit, so the next turn does not re-send the whole handbook. The trade-off is storage: a longer TTL bills more storage-hours while the customer is idle, so pick about the 90th-percentile pause, not the longest one. Think of the TTL like a parking meter: pay for the time your customers really stay. Uber also compacts at 400K tokens even on 1M-context models, so compact well before the window is full, and remember that compaction triggers come in pairs or you get a ValueError. The config classes are pydantic models with extra="forbid", so a typo such as ttl_second raises a validation error instead of being ignored. A likely question is "how do I know it works?": turn on the experimental telemetry features context_cache, compaction and token_usage and watch the counters, and note that turn 1 is never a hit. The Uber numbers come from the Uber case at the end of this chapter. Next, the Bluefin failures that make cache, compaction or memory seem to do nothing.

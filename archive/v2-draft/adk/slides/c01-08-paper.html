Two October papers show that the tool description is part of your attack surface and part of your accuracy. Step 1: in Toolflation, seller agents over-claim, so every tool says it is the best; the buyer agent stops reading and picks by list position, and task success fell from 89% to about 30% under one model. Step 2: under interface drift, a training-free validate, retry and repair layer lifted BFCL-200 accuracy from 0.580 to 0.805, and distractor tools did most of the damage. Analogy: when every product on the shelf says "number one", shoppers grab the one at eye level. Step 3: the Monday change for Acme is cheap: keep the three tools, write docstrings that say when to call, what returns and what the tool will not do, and check arguments in before_tool_callback. A likely question is "Does ADK do this validation for me?": the Literal enum and mandatory-argument checks catch some errors, but semantic checks are your callback's job.

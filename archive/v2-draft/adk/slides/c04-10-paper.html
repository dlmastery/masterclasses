Two recent papers put numbers on the cost of the graph shapes in this chapter. Top: Governance at the Boundary ran 626 KYC and AML episodes; with a 32B open model a policy fact such as a PEP match never reached the component that had to act on it in 0% of single-loop runs, 56% of pipeline runs and 85% of orchestrator-plus-subagent runs, while gpt-4.1-mini lost only 3 to 6%. It is a relay race: every handoff is a chance to drop the baton, and a single runner cannot drop it. Bottom: When Messages Go Wrong ran over 29,000 executions; 20.7% of failures gave no immediate signal, and under faults flat topologies kept 53.0% success against about 3% for sequential and hierarchical ones, when another path kept the evidence. The Monday change for Northwind: every fan-out branch reads the original claim, and facts travel as typed state checked by a deterministic node. A likely question: "So should I never use an orchestrator?" Use one when the eval says it pays, but with small models keep compliance-critical facts in one agent or in typed state. The last slide turns the chapter into checks for a design review.

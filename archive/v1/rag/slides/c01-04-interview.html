Chapter 01 · Why RAG Interview lens recall first

Interview: when RAG, when fine-tuning, and where to look first

When do you choose RAG over fine-tuning?
Use RAG for fresh, private or permissioned facts and for citations. An update is a re-index, not a retrain. Use fine-tuning for behaviour, format and style. Combine them when you need both.
Explain RAG-Sequence vs RAG-Token.
RAG-Sequence uses one retrieved document for the whole output. RAG-Token can use a different document for each token. Both marginalize over the top-k documents. On NQ, RAG-Sequence scored 44.5 EM and RAG-Token 44.1.
Your RAG bot gives wrong answers. What do you check first?
Check recall@k on a golden set. If the gold chunk is not in the top-k, the generator cannot fix the answer. Separate retrieval failures from generation failures, then fix the stage that fails.
Numbers to know.
Lewis 2020: NQ exact match 44.5 with retrieval vs 34.5 for closed-book T5-11B. Hot-swap: 70% with the matched index vs 4% mismatched. Anthropic Contextual Retrieval plus reranking: top-20 failures 5.7% → 1.9% (−67%).

Read each question aloud and give students ten seconds to answer before you press. Q1 is the most common screening question: the strong answer separates knowledge (retrieve it) from behaviour (fine-tune it) and says that the two combine. Q2 tests whether the student read the original paper; the key phrase is "marginalize over the top-k". Q3 is the debugging question: a strong candidate measures retrieval on its own before touching the prompt. Q4 lists numbers that show depth in one sentence each. Analogy for Q1: fine-tuning teaches the doctor how to talk to patients; retrieval hands the doctor today's lab results. Likely follow-up: "How do you build the golden set?" Collect real user questions, label the chunk that answers each one, and keep the set fixed so that you can compare runs.

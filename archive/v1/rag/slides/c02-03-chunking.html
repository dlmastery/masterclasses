Why chunk: embedders take 2K–32K tokens, one vector averages away detail, context costs money, precision needs passage-level units.

StrategyHow it cuts · trade-off
Fixed-sizeEvery N tokens, overlap O. Fast; cuts sentences and tables.
Recursive\n\n → \n → ". " → " " until ≤ N. Strong default; ignores meaning.
StructureMarkdown/HTML headers, code AST, by_title. Needs a good parser.

One short text passes through four strategies. The first paragraph is the Berlin passage from the late-chunking paper; S1 to S9 are its sentences in three paragraphs. Step 1: fixed-size cuts every 16 tokens, and every cut lands inside a sentence. Step 2: recursive splitting tries paragraph breaks first, so the cuts move to paragraph ends. Step 3: semantic chunking embeds each sentence and cuts where the similarity of neighbours dips below the breakpoint; it finds a topic change inside paragraph 2 that the recursive splitter misses. Step 4: the query matches child S5; parent-child indexes small sentences for precise matching but sends the whole parent paragraph to the LLM. Step 5: the Chroma numbers. Recall changes by only a few points, but IoU changes by 2x, so token waste is the bigger difference. Analogy: cutting a newspaper with a ruler versus cutting it along the articles. Likely question: "Why not always use the LLM chunker, since it has the best recall?" Answer: one LLM call per document section, slower ingestion, and its IoU is among the worst.

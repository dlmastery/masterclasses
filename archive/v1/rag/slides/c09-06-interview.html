Faithfulness is 0.95, but users still complain. What is wrong?
Faithfulness checks support, not usefulness or completeness. A grounded answer can miss the question. Measure answer relevance and retrieval recall@k on a golden set. Then check the judge against human labels for position and verbosity bias.
Your agent reads email and can send email. How do you stop indirect injection?
Treat retrieved text as data: mark it with Spotlighting. Split control from data as in CaMeL: the plan comes only from the user's query. Give tools least privilege. Ask for human confirmation before a send. Filter egress and never auto-render external URLs or images.
Pre-filter or post-filter for ACLs?
Pre-filter inside the ANN and BM25 queries, with the end user's identity, before ranking and fusion. A post-filter can return fewer than k results and leaks through counts. Partition caches and memories by the same identity.
How many human labels do you need to trust an LLM judge?
A few hundred, with Prediction-Powered Inference to get confidence intervals (ARES). Start with 100–300 labels. Recalibrate when the judge model, the prompt or the data changes.
Ask each question and let the students answer before you reveal the model answer. Q1 tests whether they separate the three triad scores: faithfulness only says the claims have evidence. Q2 is a common agent-security question at Google and Anthropic; the strong answer names architecture (CaMeL, least privilege, confirmation) and not only a better prompt. Q3: listen for "end user's identity" and "inside the query"; a service account or a post-filter is a red flag. Q4: the number is a few hundred labels with PPI confidence intervals, and the judge needs recalibration after any change. Analogy for Q4: you calibrate a new thermometer against a reference before you trust it, and again after you repair it.

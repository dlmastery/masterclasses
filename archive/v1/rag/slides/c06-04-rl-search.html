Search-R1 · UIUC, UMass, Google Cloud AI Research

  • The policy writes <search> when it needs evidence. BM25, E5 dense or web search returns <information>.
  • The policy picks how many hops to make.
  • Tokens in <information> are masked from the loss. The model does not learn to write evidence.
  • Reward = exact match of the answer, plus a format check. PPO, GRPO or REINFORCE on veRL.
  • R1-Searcher (arXiv 2503.05592): two-stage outcome RL. Stage 1 rewards correct search calls and format; stage 2 rewards the answer. No SFT cold start.
  • ReSearch (Baichuan, arXiv 2503.19470, NeurIPS 2025): search inside the reasoning chain; it generalizes across datasets. DeepResearcher (arXiv 2504.03160) trains on the live open web.

Read the rollout as a transcript the policy writes. It thinks, emits a search, gets passages back from the environment, thinks again and makes a second hop, then answers. Step 3 is the key design choice: the retrieved tokens are masked from the loss, so the gradient trains the decisions and the reasoning, not imitation of Wikipedia text. Step 4: the only reward is exact match on the final answer plus a format check, and GRPO or PPO updates the policy; nobody wrote a rule for when to search. Analogy: you grade a student only on the final answer of an open-book exam, and the student learns by itself when to open the book. Quote the numbers with the paper version: v4/v5 report +24% for the 7B model and +20% for 3B over RAG baselines; v1 said +41%. Likely question: "What can go wrong with the reward?" A format reward can be hacked: the policy can learn to emit valid tags without good answers, so keep exact match as the main signal and read sampled rollouts.

Interview: LLM datasets and synthetic data

Why might verifying distilled SFT data not improve the student?
With a strong teacher, most traces are already good. Wrong but well-formed traces still teach format and reasoning style. OpenThoughts found no significant gain from verification. Spend the verification budget on RL rewards and code, where a wrong signal does damage.
How do you stop a synthetic data loop from collapsing the model?
Keep the real data and add to it; never replace it. Flag provenance. Control diversity with personas and sampling. Dedup synthetic outputs against each other. Verify with programs. Track distinct-n and tail coverage on the card.
Design a quality filter for 10T web tokens.
A large LLM labels about 0.5M pages on a rubric. Train a small classifier (fastText, or an embedding + linear head). Set the threshold by F1 on a held-out split (FineWeb-Edu: ≥ 3, F1 82%). Log the removal rate per filter. Check the classifier for bias.
Dedup globally or per snapshot?
Measure; do not assume. FineWeb found that per-snapshot dedup (20T tokens) scored better than global dedup (about 4T tokens). Global dedup removed too much good repeated content and kept the low-quality tail.

Ask each question and let the room answer before you show the model answer. Q1 tests whether the candidate knows that verification is not free and not always useful; strong answers separate SFT distillation from RL and code. Q2 tests the collapse mechanism: replacement loses the tails, accumulation keeps them, and diversity and provenance make it measurable. Q3 is a system design question; look for the two-stage pattern of an expensive labeler and a cheap classifier, a threshold chosen on held-out data, and audit logs per filter. Q4 is a trap: the intuitive answer, "dedup everything globally", is wrong at FineWeb scale. Analogy for Q4: if you keep only one copy of every popular textbook page, the rare low-quality pages get a larger share of the data. A likely follow-up: "How do you check that the classifier is not biased?" Read samples per score band, compare slices by domain and language, and note known biases (FineWeb-Edu leans to grade-school content) on the card.

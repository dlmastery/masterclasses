Interview: labels, noise and leakage

Raw agreement is 92% but Cohen's κ is 0.3. Who is right?
Both numbers are correct. With a skewed class prior, the chance agreement pe is high, so κ = (po − pe)/(1 − pe) is low. This is the kappa paradox. Report both numbers, look at the agreement on the minority class, and use Krippendorff's α if raters skip items.
Your medical imaging model scores 0.97 AUC. What do you check first?
Patient-level leakage. Several scans per patient can sit in both folds. Re-run with GroupKFold on the patient id. Fit all preprocessing inside a Pipeline. Then search for near-duplicate images across the splits.
How do you use an LLM as a labeler safely?
Experts label a gold set of 200–500 items. Compare LLM–human α with human–human α. Swap answer orders, fix the label set, set temperature 0, and use several judges. Send low-confidence items to humans. Audit the merged labels with cleanlab. Re-audit 2–5% of the LLM labels each week.
How do you show that your RL gains are not contamination?
Decontaminate the reward prompts against every eval with n-grams, MinHash and embeddings. Score a private holdout dated after the training cutoff. Test on a fresh clone such as GSM1k, or on LiveCodeBench problems released after the cutoff. Write the method and the number removed in the quality card.
Use these as practice questions. Ask the room first, then reveal each answer. Question 1 tests whether students understand chance correction: kappa and raw agreement answer different questions, and skewed classes separate them. Question 2 is the classic leakage trap; an AUC of 0.97 on medical images should make you suspicious before it makes you happy. Question 3 tests the hybrid labeling recipe: the LLM must agree with experts as well as the experts agree with each other. Question 4 links this chapter to the RL chapter: verifiable rewards are only honest if the reward problems are not the benchmark problems. Analogy for question 4: a student who trained on last year's exam must sit a new exam to prove the skill. A likely follow-up: "What if the closed model's training data is unknown?" Answer: you cannot prove absence, so use dated, private or freshly written test items.

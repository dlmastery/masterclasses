Mitigations, cheapest first

  1. Use PEFT. Keep the adapter separate and hot-swappable.
  2. Lower the LR. Train 1–3 epochs. Stop early on held-out general benchmarks.
  3. Watch the loss: below 0.2 usually means overfitting. Scale an over-fit adapter by α × 0.5 (Unsloth).
  4. Merge back toward the base model.

Real example: SmolLM3

A linear merge of 0.9 × the APO soup + 0.1 × a mid-training checkpoint recovered the long-context RULER scores.

DAREdrop a fraction p of the deltas, rescale by 1/(1−p); often dare_ties (2311.03099)

Distillation: a small student learns from a large teacher

Off-policy

SFT on teacher outputs, as in the DeepSeek-R1-Distill Qwen and Llama students. The student never sees its own mistakes.

On-policy (GKD, 2306.13649)

The student writes, and the teacher scores every token. This removes the train/inference mismatch.

TRL DistillationTrainer

Stable since v1.10.0 (2026-08-13). beta: 0.0 = forward KL, 0.5 = JSD, 1.0 = reverse KL (default). Teacher and student must share a vocabulary. Tool-result tokens are masked.

Catastrophic forgetting is the price of specialization. Step 1: the plot has target-task accuracy on the x-axis and general accuracy on the y-axis; the list shows the usual causes. Step 2: full fine-tuning moves far right but drops a lot; LoRA moves right with a smaller drop. The mitigations are in cost order, so try the cheap ones first. Step 3: merging pulls the full-FT point back toward the base; SmolLM3 used a 0.9 to 0.1 linear merge to win back long-context scores. The plot is illustrative, not measured. Step 4: TIES in three moves: trim the small deltas, elect a sign per weight, and average only the values that agree. Step 5: distillation. On-policy distillation lets the student make its own mistakes and get a grade on every token. Analogy: off-policy is copying the teacher's homework; on-policy is the teacher marking your own homework line by line. Likely question: "Why does TIES elect a sign?" Because a plus and a minus delta on the same weight cancel when averaged, and both tasks lose.

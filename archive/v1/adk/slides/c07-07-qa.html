Interview drill: pick and justify a pattern for a real product

When does a multi-agent design beat one agent with tools?
For high-value, breadth-first, read-heavy work that splits into parallel parts and has a strong verifier. Expect about 15× the tokens of a chat. Keep the writes in one thread. Anthropic: +90.2% over single-agent Opus 4. Cognition (2026): "writes stay single-threaded".
Design a deep-research agent in ADK 2.0.
A planner node with output_schema → a dynamic fan-out of worker agents with asyncio.gather(ctx.run_node(...)), each with include_contents="none" and a short summary → a synthesizer → a citation or verifier node. Add an optional RequestInput to approve the plan. Bound the depth and max_llm_calls. Keep the plan in state.
How do you stop an LLM router from inventing a branch?
Give the router a Literal[...] field in output_schema. Route with a function on that typed field. Add a DEFAULT_ROUTE catch-all. Or use ClassifierNode with a calibrated threshold.
Why separate the evaluator from the generator?
Self-evaluation is lenient: in Anthropic's harness study, a solo run gave a broken game, and a separate Playwright evaluator gave a working app. A separate, ideally deterministic, verifier gives objective feedback. The loop converges only as fast as its verifier is trustworthy.
Read each question aloud and give students twenty seconds before you reveal the answer. Q1 tests cost awareness: the strongest answers name the 15x token cost and the read-versus-write split. Q2 is a whiteboard design; look for typed plans, isolated workers, a bound on depth and a verifier at the end. Q3 tests graph engineering: constrain the label space, then route in code. Q4 links back to the verifier's rule. Analogy for Q4: you do not let a contractor sign off their own building inspection. A common follow-up: "What would you measure in CI?" Trajectory score, task success, and the efficiency criteria, such as inference_call_count_v1 and token_usage_v1, per eval case.

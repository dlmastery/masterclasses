Rubric judges, simulated users and pass^k turn evals into a release gate

Eval-driven development: the eval suite is the spec

  • Start with 20 to 50 tasks taken from real failures.
  • Use one vocabulary: task, trial, grader, transcript, outcome.
  • Grade the outcome: the reservation exists in the database. Add trajectory checks only where the path matters.
  • Capability evals start low. Regression evals stay near 100%.
  • Read the transcripts. A 0% pass@100 usually means a broken task, not a weak agent.

pass@k: at least one of k trials succeeds. pass^k: all k trials succeed. At 75% per trial, pass^3 = 0.75³ ≈ 42%. A customer-facing agent needs pass^k.

Rubric judges: judge_model_options: {judge_model, num_samples: 5} samples the judge five times and takes the majority vote. Per-case rubrics merge with criterion rubrics.

eval_config.json · multi_run_config

{
  "criteria": {"tool_trajectory_avg_score": 1.0,
               "response_match_score": 0.8},
  "multi_run_config": {
    "num_runs": 3,
    "cross_run_scoring_by_metric": {
      "response_match_score": "min_over_runs",
      "tool_trajectory_avg_score":
        {"method": "pass_rate", "threshold": 0.9}
    }
  }
}

min_over_runs: every run must pass (pass^k). pass_rate: some flaky runs are allowed. max_crashed_runs defaults to 0.

  • Tier 1, every PR: deterministic checks and trajectory.
  • Tier 2, nightly or on release: rubric judges, multi-run.
  • Tier 3, online: judges on sampled production traffic.

Judge scores are noisy. Gate on the delta against the main baseline (agents-cli eval compare). Keep absolute floors for safety and must-pass cases.

Do not change thresholds and prompts in the same PR.

Simulated users test the conversation, not one turn

  • ConversationScenario = starting_prompt + conversation_plan + user_persona (NOVICE, EXPERT or custom).
  • The simulator stops at its stop signal or at max_allowed_invocations (default 20).
  • Reference-based criteria do not work with simulation. Use task success, rubric and hallucination metrics.
  • tau2-bench adds dual control: the user also has tools. gpt-4.1 loses 18 pp, o4-mini 25 pp.

adk optimize runs GEPA on the eval set: GEPARootAgentPromptOptimizer (default), max_metric_calls 100, reflection_minibatch_size 3. Keep a held-out validation_eval_set. GEPA: +6% against GRPO on average, up to 35× fewer rollouts.

Step 0 is Anthropic's eval-driven development: a few dozen tasks from real failures, graded on the outcome. The CORE-Bench story is the warning: the score went from 42% to 95% when the graders were fixed, so read transcripts before you blame the agent. Step 1 animates k from 1 to 10: pass@k climbs, pass^k falls; at three trials a 75% agent completes all three only 42% of the time, and multi_run_config with min_over_runs is how ADK measures that. Step 2 is the release gate: cheap deterministic checks on every PR, noisy judges on a schedule, and a block when the candidate drops more than delta below the main baseline. Step 3 runs a simulated novice user; remind students that a simulation has no fixed script, so reference-based metrics do not apply. Step 4 closes with adk optimize and the adoption gap: about half of teams run offline evals, while quality is the top barrier. Analogy: pass@k is "the archer hit the target at least once"; pass^k is "the archer hit it every time", which is what a customer feels. Likely question: "Why gate on a delta, not an absolute score?" Judge scores move with judge noise and model versions; a delta against the same-day baseline cancels much of that drift.
